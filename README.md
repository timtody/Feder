# Feder

Feder (formerly Scribe; Xcode target, module and executable `Recorder`, bundle id `de.cofinpro.recorder`; only the display name changed, so existing data stays) is a local-only iPhone app (iOS 26+) that records audio, transcribes it live on device, shows preliminary speaker labels while recording, identifies speakers accurately after the recording ends, and lets you rename speakers, tag recordings and search them. Nothing leaves the phone: audio, transcripts and speaker data stay in the app's Documents/Application Support folders.

`PIPELINE.md` is the map of what happens to a recording (both stages, every model, the measurements, the ideas on the table and what was tried and dropped); `TASKS.md` the open work; `APPSTORE.md` the checklist to the App Store (free app, tips via In-App Purchase). This README is the detailed reference.

## What it does

- **Record**: tap the red button, watch the live transcript (Apple `SpeechAnalyzer`/`SpeechTranscriber`), tap Stop. The provisional text follows in a lighter colour and firms up in place; new words are readable the moment they appear (drawn at 60 % opacity, fully opaque within 0.2 s); a small level meter beside the big timer above the Stop follows the microphone; haptics mark the presses on Transcribe and Stop. The transcript follows new text while you are at the bottom; scroll up to reread and it stops following until you tap "Jump to Live".
- **Speakers**: while recording, FluidAudio's streaming LS-EEND model labels the live transcript, the provisional words included: a new speaker starts a new paragraph at once, and the speaker's coloured name appears above it once the label has held for half a second. After Stop the WAV is diarized again on device with NVIDIA Nemotron 3 (via FluidAudio; with FluidAudio's pyannote pipeline until its 100 MB model is downloaded), which replaces the live labels (the offline result is the source of truth; the transcript card shows "Refining speakers…" meanwhile, also while it is queued right after Stop: "Try Again" only shows for a pending transcript that is not queued, or when the speaker model could not be loaded). Speakers whose voice matches a known person get that person's name. The transcript card opens each speaker turn with a name row: the speaker's name and the turn's start time (grey while preliminary). Once speakers are final, tap anywhere on a name row (full width, at least 56 pt tall, including the space above the name; it highlights and gives a light haptic) or long-press it → "Rename Speaker…": a sheet names that speaker in every turn of the transcript, by typing or by choosing one of the known people (see People and voice fingerprints). While speakers are still being identified, a tap shows a short notice under the name instead. Long-pressing the transcript's text keeps the system's text selection (iOS 27: words and ranges with Copy, Look Up, Translate; iOS 26: the whole paragraph with Copy, Share). Live labels can be switched off in Settings (Live Speaker Labels, on by default).
- **Look**: Liquid Glass. The Transcripts list is laid out like Voice Memos: a plain list on the screen's own background (white, black in dark mode), compact two-line rows (title; then date · duration · place and as many people/tag chips as fit, "+n" for the rest, laid out by `MetaLineLayout`: everything; else without the place; else fewer chips with "+n"; at last no chips and the place truncated. Not `ViewThatFits`: iOS 26 measures its candidates on SwiftUI's async render thread, where their main-actor `ForEach` closures trapped in Swift 6's isolation check; the layout is `nonisolated` and captures nothing. Titles are one line), the large "Transcripts" heading as the list's first row (not a navigation bar title: it scrolls away with the list; the navigation bar is hidden), and in the Search tab the suggestions and results start right under the search bar ("Suggested" and "n Transcripts" are caption rows, `listCaption`, not section headers, which a plain list places ~22 pt lower). Swiping a row left shows Voice Memos' wide pills with the title under them: Delete (outermost; a full swipe deletes, without a confirmation) and Share (the plain-text transcript; `ShareSheet` presents the system share sheet (the activity view controller itself, with its own fast presentation) at once, since `ShareLink` prepared its items first; a still image over the row's snap-back and a container sheet were tried and dropped, as they made the sheet appear later). These are List's own `swipeActions`; iOS picks their look by the row's height (measured on iOS 27, iPhone 17): up to 59 pt capsules with the title inside, about 62-70 pt wide pills with the title under them, from 76 pt round buttons (`UIContextualAction` has no public style for it), so rows are two lines with a minimum height of 62 pt (`minRowHeight`, set on the row content so the caption rows stay small): the pill's height follows the row's (62 pt rows: 60 × 34 pt pills, 66 pt: 60 × 38), and Voice Memos' rows are about 62 pt with 60 × 33 pt pills (measured on a screenshot of it). Rows with a search snippet are taller and get the round buttons. The row has no background of its own, so a swiped row shows no edge. **Tabs** (`Views/MainView.swift`, Apple Music's Liquid Glass layout, iOS 26): a `TabView` with Settings (gear), Transcripts (waveform) and Search (magnifier, at the right end) in one tab bar, and the record control as the tab bar's bottom accessory above it (`.tabViewBottomAccessory`, where Music has its mini player; the system draws the glass capsule). Scrolling down minimizes the bar to three items like Music's (`.tabBarMinimizeBehavior(.onScrollDown)`): the current tab, the accessory ("● Transcribe") and Search as its own circle on the right. `Tab(role: .search)` is always set apart as that circle, so Search is an ordinary tab while the bar is expanded and takes the search role only while it is minimized (`isBarMinimized`, from the accessory's `tabViewBottomAccessoryPlacement == .inline`); being last in the bar, it stays at the right end instead of jumping there from the middle. `isBarMinimized` starts `true` and the accessory resets it on its first appearance, before the first frame: the Search circle then exists from launch. Created only at the first collapse, it came late to the minimized bar's layout, and the accessory was laid out as wide as without it, running under the circle for ~0.3 s before it snapped to its width (frame by frame on the iPhone 17 simulator; every later collapse was fine). The tab bar's size is the system's, the same as Music's (no size API). The "Transcripts" and "Settings" headings are not pinned: the first list row and the first section header, they scroll away with the content (no navigation bar titles), at the same place (x 20 pt like the list's row text, same top: the section header is pulled 11 pt left and 5 pt up); the Search tab has no heading. Leaving the Search tab puts the keyboard away (MainView resigns the first responder: the tab's view stays alive, so its focus state alone did not). A row's date line is one VoiceOver element with all of it (date, duration, place, people, tags) and a "Filter by tag" action per tag: `MetaLineLayout` parks what doesn't fit off-screen, where it stayed in the accessibility tree. **Transcript card** (`Views/TranscriptCard.swift`, Apple Music's "Now Playing"): a transcript is always shown in one full-screen card, the same surface for recording and for viewing, never pushed. The card is MainView's one `fullScreenCover` with a zoom transition (`.navigationTransition(.zoom)`; sources are marked with `.cardSource(id)`, `matchedTransitionSource` in MainView's namespace): out of the record accessory for a recording, out of the tapped row for a transcript from the Transcripts list, the Search results or Settings › People › person (all through the `openTranscript` environment action; the row ids are scoped by list, `OpenTranscriptAction.rowSource`, because every tab stays alive and the same transcript is a row in several lists at once). It covers the tab bar and the accessory. At the top left a glass back button (`CardCloseButton`, a 44 pt circle with a chevron, where a pushed page has its Back and where Photos puts it over a zoomed photo; VoiceOver "Minimize"): tapping it, or swiping the card to the right from the left screen edge (`CardSwipeToClose`: UIKit's own back gesture, a `UIScreenEdgePanGestureRecognizer`, so the card follows the finger and closes past a third of the width or a flick; scroll views, text selection and the tag chips' horizontal scroll give way to an edge pan as they do in every navigation stack) shrinks it back to where it came from: during a recording into the accessory (recording goes on), a transcript into its row. Swiping down only scrolls the content: the zoom transition's own interactive dismissal is off (`interactiveDismissDisabled`), because pulling the card down competed with scrolling the transcript (the earlier grabber). The content starts 60 pt down and scrolls under the button's glass (no band, no fade). No top bar: nothing at the top right. While recording the card shows `RecordView`, laid out like Voice Memos' recording sheet and exactly like the transcript it becomes: at the top the name the transcript will get ("New Transcript N", fixed when the session is made at Record, `RecordingSession.transcriptName`, so the title field never shows its "Title" placeholder while the card zooms in) as a title and its date line (the start time, then " · 📍 place" fading in once `PlaceLocator` has it, `RecordingSession.place`), same top offset, margin, fonts and positions as the transcript's (a disabled-looking but real `TextField`: a `Text` sat 1 pt higher); the live transcript below; at the bottom the level meter beside a big 44 pt timer, "<language> · On device" and the large Stop. When Stop saves something the same card turns into the transcript in place (no push, no new page; `TranscriptCard.Turn`), starting at Stop, not when saving has finished (Stop → saved measured 120–180 ms on the simulator, 70 ms of it stopping the audio engine): title and date line stay where they are (the transcript's lie exactly over them; a pixel diff of the last recording frame and the first transcript frame differs only by antialiasing, ≤ 15 grey levels); the big timer shrinks and flies into the date line's duration slot while the place moves aside for it (0.45 s, the timer's text is the saved duration, so it lands on the transcript's digits; a place that fits beside the date alone but not beside date and duration moves down to the second line as the slot opens, `DateLineLayout`); the live text, meter and caption fade out (0.2 s); and the large Stop's Liquid Glass melts into the transcript's bottom toolbar. The melt: the system bar can't morph from a 72 pt disc (a bar item is at most a 48 pt glass; only a regular-glass capsule ≥ ~92 pt wide morphed into the bar's group, a round or `.glassProminent` Stop just faded, and Delete never split off), so two glass shapes in a `GlassEffectContainer` start on top of each other on the Stop (one blob) and spring apart onto the bar's measured frames (a 0.45 s spring with bounce 0.25 computed per frame and eased onto its end at exactly 0.55 s: SwiftUI's `.bouncy` with completion `.removed` only completed 1.0–1.4 s after Stop, so the bar looked finished for over half a second before its buttons worked; the real bar now takes over ~0.7 s after Stop) (Share + Add Tag + Speakers capsule 28, 798, 175 × 48, 116 wide before Add Tag, and Delete 326, 798, 48 × 48 on a 402 × 874 pt iPhone, from the system's glass and image views; another item in that group needs measuring again; 28 pt from the screen edges, 42 pt above the safe area's bottom), the red square shrinking away within 0.1 s (slower, it hung as a pink dot in the gap between the two shapes) and the bar's symbols (17 pt medium, large) fading in above the container (inside it they took on the glass's vibrancy). Then the real toolbar appears under them in one frame without animation, and they go in that frame: the transcript view was inserted with its bar hidden at least 50 ms before (shown in the same update that inserts its view, iOS materialised it: an empty frame, then ~230 ms of blur), and until then a 52 pt near-transparent bottom `safeAreaBar` stands in for the bar's soft scroll edge (`Color.clear` gave none). The replicas take no touches and are hidden from VoiceOver; users and tests reach only the real toolbar. Only where the bar was measured (iOS 27, Face ID iPhones, text below the accessibility sizes); elsewhere, and with Reduce Motion, the Stop fades out, the timer fades out while the duration fades in at its place, and the bar fades in. The timer's flight, the fades and the melt are drawn frame by frame (`TimelineView`), not with SwiftUI animations, on the turn's own clock (`TurnClock`): the frames' time from the first frame drawn after Stop, each step at most 1/30 s. Right after Stop the main thread is busy for ~0.1 s (saving, inserting the transcript), and an implicit fade only started after that; timed by the wall clock from Stop, the first frame seen was already a tenth of a second in (the live text half faded, the timer 24 pt up), a cut rather than a fade. On the turn's clock a stall only holds the motion up. The melt's glass also stands by over the Stop while recording (the same layer at 1 % opacity): inserted only at Stop, the render server needed ~0.1 s for its first frame. The transcript is often saved within 0.15 s, so the recording's title, date line and timer stay on top until the timer has landed and the melt has settled. The transcript's summary, tags and text fade in (0.25 s) as the live text has faded out, into the space it left (a crossfade showed live and saved paragraphs at once; starting it as the timer landed left the body empty for ~0.25 s). If the recording is discarded after all while turning, the melt fades and the card closes as it stands; a failure crossfades to its screen. A card opened while the recording is being saved (Stop, swiped down, "Saving…" tapped) turns too. The saved transcript carries what the card showed: `createdAt` is the recording's start, and the place is written before saving when it was found already (otherwise once it is). The live text is frozen at Stop (`RecordingSession.show`), so words finalized while saving don't re-split its paragraphs just before; when the speakers are refined later the transcript's paragraphs change with an animation. **Type**: the transcript's text, live and saved, and the summary (also while it is written) are set in the typeface chosen in Settings › Reading (`TranscriptFont`): New York by default, the system serif, as in Books, a document to read rather than an interface; SF Pro; or Libron, a reading font by Nico Verbruggen made for e-readers (a calmer revision of Readerly), bundled under the SIL Open Font License (v0.25 from github.com/nicoverbruggen/libron, the four TTFs unchanged and the license text in `Recorder/Fonts`, registered through `UIAppFonts`; nothing is downloaded). The options are rows with a checkmark, each name drawn in its own typeface (a menu picker can't draw custom fonts). Dynamic Type and bold (search hits) work in all three. Everything else stays SF Pro: title, date line, speaker names and times, tags, and the chrome the system draws (tab bar, toolbars, search field, swipe pills, menus), which no font setting reaches. The live text has it too, since it turns into the saved transcript in place at Stop. The transcript (`Views/RecordingDetailView.swift`): the title (editable), date · duration · place (`TranscriptDateLine`), summary, tags, speakers and the final transcript, and the system's bottom toolbar (`.bottomBar` toolbar items, Liquid Glass): Share Transcript (the same `ShareSheet` as the list's Share swipe action, so both show the transcript's title and the app symbol in the sheet's header) Add Tag (inserts the tag line, see Organize) and Speakers (a menu with the speaker-count picker: Automatic, 2–6 Speakers) in one glass group, Delete Transcript (trash, asks first: the confirmation is attached to the toolbar button, so it comes out of it as in Photos and Notes; the card closes, then the transcript is deleted once the view has disappeared, since reading a deleted @Model traps) on the right; opened from a search result, "n of m" with Previous Match/Next Match as a group between them (see Search). With search hits a second flexible spacer made the bar too wide for an iPhone 17 (iOS moved Next and Delete into an overflow "…" menu), so Delete follows the hits after a fixed gap; while hits are shown, Share, Add Tag and Speakers share one "More" menu (ellipsis) on every iPhone, since seven items fit no iPhone's bar (see Search), so the navigator and Delete stay visible. A hit is scrolled to the centre only when it isn't wholly on screen (like Safari's Find on Page), so a result whose first hit is near the top opens with the title in place. The transcript runs on under the bottom toolbar with the system's soft scroll edge (`scrollEdgeEffectStyle(.soft, for: .bottom)`), as in Notes and Mail and as the live view has under its Stop. The back button floats over the top left of the transcript (an overlay; the content starts 60 pt lower, `contentMargins`, and scrolls under the glass). Not a top `safeAreaBar` with the system's scroll edge effect: on iOS 27 that switched off the paragraphs' text selection in the card (a long press selected nothing and showed no Copy; found by `testLongPressMenusKeepTextSelection`, and gone with the overlay). What the card shows (`TranscriptCardContent`: the session, the transcript, the search context, the zoom source) is an observable object the card reads itself: as plain state read only in the cover's content closure, the cover came up blank and zoomed back into the wrong source. It stays until the cover's `onDismiss`, so the card keeps its content and its zoom while it shrinks; only then a session that ended (saved, discarded, failed) is dropped. Stop in the accessory while the card is minimized opens the card on the saved transcript; a discarded recording closes the card. A request to open the card while it is still shrinking away (a row or Transcribe tapped, or the recording saved during the zoom back) waits until its `onDismiss` (`MainView.pendingCard`) and then opens it, so the dismissal neither wipes the new content nor zooms the old card into the new source. A recording saved while the card shows another transcript waits in the list, and the tab behind that card (its zoom source) stays as it is. The session is started at once on the tap on Transcribe: the audio session and the engine come up on the `AudioHardware` actor, never on the main thread (started there, the card only came up after "Preparing…", which a 120 ms delay used to paper over), and "Preparing…" is shown only once preparing has taken 0.4 s (the first load of the speech model); normally the card opens straight onto the empty transcript. The haptics come with the presses themselves (Transcribe: medium, Stop: light), not with the state changes a moment later.
The accessory (`Views/RecordAccessory.swift`, Music's mini player sizes): without a recording a solid red dot and "Transcribe"; while recording a pulsing red dot, the time, the microphone level (`LevelMeter`) and Stop (VoiceOver: "Recording" with the elapsed time as its value); "Preparing…", "Saving…" and "Can't Record" in between. ## Architecture

**Modules.** Three layers, one direction: `Views → Services → Models`. `Models/` holds the SwiftData models (`Recording`, `Person`) and pure, `nonisolated` value code (alignment, live labels, search, clustering, voice windows). `Services/` owns the work: one `RecordingSession` per recording, the app-wide `SpeakerIdentifier` queue after Stop, `PeopleDirectory` (voice fingerprints), the model-owning engines (speech, Parakeet, Nemotron, pyannote/WeSpeaker, LS-EEND, Gemma) and their downloads, and the search index. `Views/` is SwiftUI only. Nothing under `Models/` or `Services/` imports SwiftUI; `FluidAudio` is imported only by the services that run its models (`FluidAudioDiarizer`, `NemotronDiarizer`, `VoiceEmbedder`, `LSEENDLiveDiarizer`, `ParakeetTranscriber`), MLX only by `LocalLanguageModel`.

**Who owns what.** `AppServices` (the composition root, in the environment) owns the SwiftData container, `SpeakerIdentifier`, `PeopleDirectory`, the live diarizer and the three model downloads, and makes a fresh `RecordingSession` per recording. `MainView` owns the UI's few pieces of shared state: the selected tab, `SearchState` (the Search tab's text and tokens, shared with the Transcripts tab's pull-to-search and tag chips), `isBarMinimized` (the tab bar's state, reported by the accessory), and the card: `TranscriptCardContent` (the session, the transcript, the search context, the zoom source), `showsCard` and `isCardDismissing`/`pendingCard` (a request that came while the card was still shrinking away). The active `RecordingSession` lives in the card's content, so it survives the card being swiped away; the accessory and the card read it. Every other view owns only its own `@State`; the lists read SwiftData through `@Query`; the transcript card edits its `Recording` in place (title, tags) and calls the services for everything else (rename → `PeopleDirectory.name`, "Speakers: n" → `SpeakerIdentifier.setSpeakerCount`, delete). Views get what they need from the environment: `AppServices`, the model context, `openTranscript` (opens the card out of a row: `OpenTranscriptAction`, `.cardSource`), `searchActions` (open/close the Search tab) and the zoom namespace. All of it is `@Observable`; `@Observable` services publish only what views read (`SpeakerIdentifier.inFlight/refining/modelError`, `BetterSummaries.state`, the download states), the rest is `@ObservationIgnored`.

**Data flow of a recording.** Transcribe → `MainView.startRecording` makes the session, puts it in the card's content (the accessory shows "Preparing…" at once, the card zooms in) and starts it. `RecordingSession.start`: microphone permission → `AudioCapture.activateSession` → `Transcriber.prepare` (model load) → `AudioCapture.start` (file + analyzer stream + live samples + levels) → `Transcriber.start`; `.recording`. While recording, the transcriber's events and the live diarizer's snapshots arrive on the main actor and update the pure `LiveTranscript`, whose `LiveText` the card draws (`LiveTranscriptView`, incremental). Stop → `seal` → the transcriber's last words (≤ 2 s) → the `Recording` is inserted and saved → `.finished` → `SpeakerIdentifier.enqueue` (words, speakers, voices, recognition; then the vocabulary pass and the title on its background queue). The card turns into the transcript when `.finishing` is seen (`TranscriptCard.Turn`), and `MainView` swaps the content when `.finished` arrives.

**Concurrency domains** (Swift 6, `SWIFT_DEFAULT_ACTOR_ISOLATION = MainActor`, so everything is main-actor unless marked):
- *Main actor:* the views, `RecordingSession`, `SpeakerIdentifier` (its queues are main-actor tasks that await the workers), `PeopleDirectory`, all SwiftData access (one `mainContext`), `TranscriptSearchModel`'s bookkeeping and the segments reads (in batches of 8 recordings between frames).
- *`AudioHardware` (global actor):* `MicrophoneCapture`: AVAudioSession activation/deactivation, AVAudioEngine start/stop. The tap thread touches only the `Mutex`-guarded `CaptureTap`, whose closures are built in `nonisolated` statics (a main-actor closure literal traps on the render thread).
- *Worker tasks owning a model:* `NemotronDiarizer`, `FluidAudioDiarizer` (+ `VoiceEmbedder`), `LSEENDLiveDiarizer`: one detached task each, requests through an `AsyncStream`, only `Sendable` values cross. `LocalLanguageModel` is an actor (MLX on the GPU). `SpeechAnalyzer` is Apple's actor. Parakeet runs on FluidAudio's executors (`AsrManager` is an actor).
- *Global executor (`@concurrent`):* the search index's folding and queries (`TranscriptSearch`), `DiarizerSelector.regroup` (the "Speakers: n" clustering). Under `NonisolatedNonsendingByDefault` a plain `nonisolated async` function runs on its caller's actor, so off-main work is marked `@concurrent` explicitly.
- *Possibly off the main thread by SwiftUI:* `Layout`s (`MetaLineLayout`, `DateLineLayout`, `WrappingLayout`) and `TextRenderer`s (`WordFadeRenderer`) are `nonisolated` and capture nothing: iOS 26 measures them on the render thread, where a main-actor closure trapped.

**Deliberately simple.** State is derived where it can be: `RecordView` reads the timer's end from the session (`recordedDuration`, set together with `.finishing`), the card's melt eligibility is one expression, the lists hold no closures (so SwiftUI skips them when `MainView` updates). The only state machines are `RecordingSession.State` and `SpeakerIdentifier`'s two queues; the card's `Turn` holds what happens between Stop and the toolbar hand-over.

**The five service protocols** (`Services/ServiceContracts.swift`):

- `AudioCapture` (nonisolated, Sendable; `MicrophoneCapture` runs on the `AudioHardware` global actor): `activateSession()`, `start(writingTo:analyzerFormat:liveSamples:levels:) -> AsyncStream<AnalyzerInput>`, `seal() -> TimeInterval` (synchronous, for the main actor at Stop: finishes the streams and closes the WAV) and `shutDown()` (removes the tap, stops the engine, deactivates the session, off the main actor). Every converted buffer is both written to the WAV and yielded with its cumulative frame position as `bufferStartTime`, so file time, word time and diarizer time share one origin. With a `liveSamples` continuation the same frames are also resampled by one persistent converter to mono Float32 at `LiveAudio.sampleRate` (8 kHz) and yielded in order, never dropped. With a `levels` continuation each buffer's RMS level (-50 dBFS → 0 … -10 dBFS → 1) is yielded for the meter (`bufferingNewest(1)`, about 10 per second). On iOS 27 the tap uses `installAudioTap(onBus:bufferSize:format:tapProvider:)`; everything the tap thread touches sits in one Mutex-guarded `CaptureTap`.
- `Transcriber` (MainActor): `prepare(locale:onDownloadProgress:)` (also loads the model: `prepareToAnalyze` runs before the microphone starts), `start(input:onEvent:)`, `finish()`. Events (`.volatile`, `.finalized([TranscriptWord])`, `.failed`) arrive on the main actor.
- `FileTranscriber` (MainActor): `transcribe(fileURL:locale:) -> FileTranscript` (the words after Stop with confidences and their `alignmentOffset`: seconds added to the diarizer's turn times when they are aligned with these words, -0.12 for Parakeet, 0 for Apple; the stored word times stay unchanged) and `applyVocabulary(to:confidences:fileURL:locale:)` (the vocabulary pass; every word keeps its times).
- `SpeakerDiarizer` (nonisolated, Sendable): `prepare()`, `diarization(fileURL:speakers:) -> Diarization` (exclusive turns, speaker indices 0-based in first-appearance order, and which model made them; `speakers` is `.automatic` or `.exactly(n)`) and `voices(fileURL:windows:) -> [Int: SpeakerVoice]` (each speaker's unit WeSpeaker embedding, from VoiceWindows).
- `LiveDiarizer` (nonisolated, Sendable): `prepare()` and `start(samples:) -> AsyncStream<LiveSpeakerTurns>`. Each snapshot is the full state so far (turns in seconds, speaker indices 0-based by first appearance, plus `processedUntil`), at most one per 0.5 s of audio, plus a last one after the samples ended. Best effort: a recording never waits for it.

**RecordingSession** (`@MainActor @Observable`, one instance per recording from `AppServices.makeRecordingSession()`): drives `idle → preparing → (downloadingSpeechModel) → recording → finishing → finished(Recording) | discarded | failed`. Its audio work never blocks the main thread: `MicrophoneCapture` runs on the `AudioHardware` global actor (one for the whole app: activation, engine start, engine stop and deactivation stay in Apple's order even when the next recording starts at once), and `start()` awaits it, so the card zooms in on a free main thread. Stop seals the capture synchronously (`seal()`: the streams end, the WAV closes, the duration is known in the same update as `.finishing`, so the card's timer lands on it from the first frame) and shuts the engine down off the main actor (`shutDown()`), which is why the Stop → toolbar turn now starts on the next frame (Stop → first changed frame 115–130 ms before, 50–75 ms after, on the iPhone 17 simulator; the rest is SwiftUI's update, see Performance). While recording it hands every transcription result and live-label snapshot (at most two per second) to `LiveTranscript` (pure) and publishes its `LiveText`, plus a smoothed microphone level. `LiveText` holds the finalized words in paragraphs with their live speakers (`LiveSpeakers` labels the words, `SteadyLabels` folds them into paragraphs; both incrementally, row ids stay stable), then the provisional words. SpeechTranscriber sends its provisional text as one run over everything not final yet, so `VolatileTimer` splits it into words with estimated times (words unchanged since the last result keep theirs; new ones are spread up to 0.6 s before the range end by length), and every word, provisional or final, is shown under the newest live label for its time (none yet: with the word before). A different speaker therefore starts a new paragraph at once; the speaker's name appears once the label has held for 0.5 s (`SteadyLabels.hold`; per 0.1 s of audio for provisional words, `FrameSteady`), and a refresh runs when one comes due. The live screen (`LiveTranscriptView`) places the provisional words by those labels (the rules `SteadyLabels` applies once they are final, so firming up rarely moves them) and fades in only words that are new where they stand: a `TextRenderer` draws them at 60 % opacity at once, fully opaque within 0.2 s (each word fades over 0.15 s; words arriving together start up to 0.05 s apart), without blur or offset (a `TimelineView` runs only while something fades; with Reduce Motion nothing fades). On Stop (button, audio interruption or transcriber failure) it waits at most 2 s for the transcriber's last words (`finish()` normally takes about 0.1 s, but up to 47 s were measured on a loaded Mac; past the cap the provisional tail is kept as words and later results are ignored, since the full-accuracy pass re-transcribes the file anyway) and at most 0.5 s for the live diarizer's last snapshot, saves the `Recording` with its words and segments (labelled with the preliminary live speakers when available, otherwise un-attributed; `speakerStatus` stays `pending`), then hands it to `SpeakerIdentifier`. Empty recordings are discarded and their file deleted. Interruptions: `interruptionNotification` (began) on iOS 26, `didBecomeInactiveNotification` with a system deactivation source on iOS 27.

**SpeakerIdentifier** (`@MainActor @Observable`, app-wide) runs the work after Stop on one serial queue, one recording at a time (Parakeet alone is a ~600 MB model): (1) the full-accuracy words (`FileTranscriber.transcribe`; they replace the live words at once, with the live labels re-applied; "Improving transcript accuracy…"), (2) diarization (`DiarizerSelector`, "Refining speakers…"), (3) the final alignment (`TranscriptAligner.finalSegments`: no island smoothing, the turns moved by the words' alignment offset), (4) each speaker's voice from those final labels (`VoiceWindows` → `SpeakerDiarizer.voices`), (5) recognition of known people (`PeopleDirectory.recognize`), then `.done`: the user sees the speakers. Each step's start and end goes into `SpeakerIdentifier.progress[id]` (`PipelineProgress`), the words a pass replaced into `recentChanges[id]` for 6 s, and the summary as it is written into `partialSummaries[id]`, which the card's row, flashes and summary placeholder read. The diarizer's turns are stored (`Recording.speakerTurns`, with `alignmentOffset`), so the words can be re-aligned later without diarizing again, and so is the model that made them (`Recording.diarizerRaw`: Nemotron or pyannote; recognition is stricter for pyannote's speakers, see People). Only then, on a separate background queue, the vocabulary pass (`FileTranscriber.applyVocabulary`) improves the words and re-aligns them with the stored turns: speaker numbers, names, links and voices stay valid, and `.done` never waits for it (the whole-file Dictation pass is most of the post-stop time: 5.4 of 7.4 s for 5 minutes on an M5 Mac). Then, on the same background queue, `TranscriptTitler` writes the automatic title and summary from the final words (see Automatic titles and summaries). `Recording.vocabularyPending` survives a kill, so the pass resumes at the next launch. The live labels' speaker count no longer matters: the old `.atLeast(live count)` bound and live-speaker fallback are gone (on DiarBench the live count was exact in only 44/70 recordings, and the fallback would have replaced a correct Nemotron result with live labels). The "Speakers" menu in the transcript card's bottom toolbar (Automatic, 2–6) stores `Recording.speakerCountOverride`, resets the speaker names and re-runs diarization with `.exactly(n)` (the words are kept). `prepareAndRetryPending()` runs on launch and every return to the foreground and re-queues everything still `pending` or `processing` (offline first launch, crash recovery), every unfinished vocabulary pass and, when the language model is available, every transcript not yet described (the one-time title sweep). **Order:** a recording that was just stopped always goes first: its identification (words, speakers, voices) before the next transcript of the one-time voice migration (`refreshVoices`; known names follow once the transcripts people's samples come from are migrated, see People), its vocabulary pass and title before the next transcript of the title sweep. Both sweeps take one transcript at a time and start nothing new while a recording is being made (`RecordingSession` reports Record … saved or dropped through `setRecording`); the transcript a sweep is on is finished first. Debug builds log the order (`[Scribe] queue: …`). Deletion while processing is safe (`isDeleted` checks after every await); whoever writes the segments last aligns the current words with the current turns, so the two queues never leave them inconsistent.

**TranscriptAligner** (pure, `nonisolated`): each word takes the speaker turn with the largest overlap (nearest turn midpoint when nothing overlaps); for live labels, single-word speaker flips between two agreeing neighbours are smoothed (`smoothing: true`, the default); the final alignment after Stop (`finalSpeakers` / `finalSegments`) moves the turns by the words' alignment offset and does not smooth (on DiarBench smoothing erased real one-word turns: 2.43 → 2.22 % wrong words without it, and the Parakeet offset took it to 1.77 %); consecutive same-speaker words fold into one paragraph, split additionally by silences over 2 s. With no turns every segment has `speakerIndex == nil` (live labels off, undiarized recordings). Words ending after `labelledUntil` (audio the live diarizer has not processed yet) stay unlabelled instead of guessing; `keepingIDsOf:` gives a segment the id of the previous segment that started at the same time, so the live list keeps its rows. Each set of turns is indexed once (`TurnIndex`: binary searches instead of a scan from the first turn). While recording, `LiveSpeakers` keeps the live labels up to date incrementally: a new snapshot re-assigns only the words ending after the earliest changed turn and the words in silence gaps whose nearest turn could have changed (same labels as the full pass; randomized equivalence test in the live latency harness, `LiveLat equiv`, 3.7 million states).

## Simulator vs. device

`SpeechTranscriber.isAvailable` is `false` on every simulator, so Debug builds running on the simulator inject `FakeTranscriber` (canned sentences word by word until Stop: provisional words lower case without punctuation, a pause over 2 s after every second sentence; it declares the device's Int16 analyzer format, because iOS 27's `AnalyzerInput` traps on Float32 non-interleaved buffers), `FakeLiveDiarizer` (two speakers alternating every 5 s of received audio, about 1 s behind, a snapshot every 0.5 s, so live labels appear), `FakeDiarizer` (two speakers alternating every 4 s, or `n` with "Speakers: n", so the offline pass visibly replaces the live labels, reported as Nemotron's; a fixed fake voice per speaker number, so a speaker named once is recognized in later fake recordings) and `FakeFileTranscriber` (finds no better words after 1 s; its vocabulary pass changes nothing after 2 s) under `#if DEBUG && targetEnvironment(simulator)`. The real `MicrophoneCapture` still runs against the host microphone and writes a real WAV, so the whole record → stop → detail flow can be exercised. Real speech recognition and diarization must be validated on a physical iPhone 12 or newer running iOS 26+; older devices (iPhone 11, SE 2) show "Not Available on This Device".

## After Stop: words, speakers, vocabulary

After Stop, `SpeakerIdentifier` (one recording at a time) first re-transcribes the saved WAV (`SpeechFileTranscriber.transcribe`), then identifies the speakers (next section) and marks the transcript `.done`; the vocabulary pass runs after that in the background. Better words replace the live words (live speaker labels are re-applied until diarization finishes); if the pass fails or finds nothing, the live words stay. It runs once per transcript (`Recording.wordsRefined`).

1. **The words** (before speakers). NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B "Ultra" via FluidAudio (`ParakeetTranscriber`, CoreML, on device, 25 European languages) when it is chosen in Settings (default), downloaded and supports the language; otherwise Apple's `SpeechTranscriber` without `.fastResults`. The ~610 MB model downloads once into Application Support/FluidAudio/Models, automatically on Wi-Fi only (`ParakeetModel`, NWPathMonitor), on cellular via "Download Now" in Settings. Word timestamps and confidences come from Parakeet's SentencePiece token timings.
2. **Vocabulary terms** (after speakers are shown, `applyVocabulary`, background queue; the improved words are re-aligned with the stored speaker turns, so speaker numbers and names stay). `DictationTranscriber` with the vocabulary as contextual strings, merged by `VocabularyMerge` (only terms it recognized at the same time position; case fixes for exact matches). `SpeechTranscriber` ignores contextual strings (measured: identical output with and without).
3. **Smart corrections (experimental, off by default; its own background step after the vocabulary pass, before the summary).** `TranscriptCorrector` sends the transcript's paragraphs as "Name: text" lines to Gemma 4 E2B (the Better Summaries model, so it needs that download; German transcripts only) with fixed German instructions: correct only grammar, spelling, punctuation and obviously misheard words, never content, style or wording; every line stays with its speaker, also where a sentence runs on across a change of speaker (the example shows exactly that); unintelligible passages become "[unklar]"; keep the "Name: Text" format; output only the transcript. About 10–15 speaker turns per request (`chunk`: the turns evened out over the requests, at most 6,000 characters each), since longer inputs make a small model skip lines or start summarizing; temperature 0.25 (not greedy), so it copies rather than paraphrases; the vocabulary (your words and the built-in terms) follows the instructions as a glossary (`TranscriptCorrector.instructions(glossary:)`: "so schreiben, wenn sie offensichtlich gemeint sind, aber nicht einsetzen, wo ein anderes Wort passt"), so a term the vocabulary pass could not place can still be spelled right; the review box shows where it was used. `CorrectionMerge` (pure, `Models/`) checks every answer (the same lines in the same order, names mapped one to one, so "Speaker 1" written as "Sprecher 1" throughout is fine; a line left out counts as emptied when the other names still follow in order) and aligns its words with the paragraphs' words by longest common subsequence without case and punctuation: a kept or respelled word takes the time of the word it stands for, added words share the time between their neighbours, and every word stays in the paragraph it came from, whatever line the answer wrote it into (who said what is the diarization's: it hears the voices, and a sentence finished by the other speaker isn't evidence of a wrong one; the first version of this pass appended "a fragment that belongs to the previous speaker" there, and its own example, a line left out, was rejected by the merge); an answer that changes more than 30 % of a chunk's words, more than half of a paragraph's, drops a turn or changes the word count by more than 30 % is rejected and the chunk stays. For now nothing is written into the transcript by itself: the corrected paragraphs are stored as a proposal (`Recording.proposedCorrections`) and the card shows a box under the tags (`CorrectionReview`) with one row per change (the speaker, the old words struck through, the new ones; `CorrectionMerge.changes`, an exact word diff, runs one unchanged word apart joined), a checkmark per change row that takes over just that change (`SpeakerIdentifier.acceptCorrection`: the words replaced, times kept, paragraphs as they are), "Apply All" (the whole proposal) and "Dismiss". The rows are the live difference between transcript and proposal, so an accepted row disappears and the last one ends the review; the changed words flash as the vocabulary pass's do. Every answered chunk extends the proposal at once, so an interrupted pass (the app resigns active: Gemma only runs in the foreground; a recording starts) goes on where it stopped (`Recording.correctedUntil`, `correctionsPending`). The summary is written from the unreviewed text. Not measured yet on real recordings. The first version used Apple's Foundation Models model on single suspicious words with code-side checks; it did not help on real speech (below), which is why the setting is off by default.

Measured on 80 real German clips with reference transcripts (40 phone calls to a bank from PolyAI MINDS-14, 624 words; 40 audiobook excerpts from Multilingual LibriSpeech, 1282 words), on macOS 27. Word error rates count formatting differences as errors ("1 km" vs "einem Kilometer", "gebe" vs "geb"), so character error rates (letters only, no spaces) are given too:

| Step 1 engine | Phone calls WER | Audiobooks WER | Audiobooks CER |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apple SpeechTranscriber | 11.1 % | 11.0 % | 4.4 % |
| Parakeet v3 | 12.3 % | 14.7 % | – |
| **Parakeet Ultra** (app default) | 11.1 % | 8.9 % | 2.4 % |
| Apple + smart corrections (first version, Apple's model) | 11.2 % | 11.2 % | – |

Parakeet Ultra transcribed the 15.5 minutes of audio in about 6 s on an M-series Mac (Apple's pass: several times longer). An earlier test with macOS's synthetic German voices showed 20 % WER and large LLM gains; those voices (all except "Anna") are formant synthesizers the recognizers handle badly, and the LLM gains came from fixing their garbled non-words. Real speech showed neither.

The vocabulary (Transcripts screen, Settings) holds your own words plus an optional built-in list of German consulting, banking and finance terms (`Models/Vocabulary.swift`). The live transcript does not use it.

## Automatic titles and summaries

After the vocabulary pass, on the same background queue (`SpeakerIdentifier.describe`, never before `.done`), `TranscriptTitler` asks an on-device language model for a 2-6 word title and a short summary in the transcript's language: Apple's (Foundation Models, `LanguageModelSession`, greedy sampling, at most 400 answer tokens; 1-2 sentences), or Gemma 4 E2B with Better Summaries (see below; 2-3 short sentences). The model reads the transcript as "Name: text" lines (the speaker's name, "Speaker N" while unnamed), and is told to name people but never write "Speaker N" (Gemma: "ein Teilnehmer"/"a participant") (NaturalLanguage's guess when it is > 80 % sure, else the recording's locale). The answer is a `@Generable` `TitleAndSummary` (topics, then summary, then title: listing the topics first makes a long meeting's summary cover more than its first part). When the model refuses (`.refusal` / `.guardrailViolation`, seen for "ich möchte gerne Geld auf mein Konto einzahlen"), it is asked once more as plain text under `.permissiveContentTransformations` guardrails ("Summary: …" / "Title: …" lines). iOS 26 reports a refusal as `LanguageModelSession.GenerationError`, iOS 27 as `LanguageModelError` (a build with the 27 SDK gets only the new type on a 27 system: on macOS 27 a guardrail violation arrived as `LanguageModelError`, which a `catch GenerationError` misses), so `TranscriptTitler.isRefusal` checks both; with it the plain-text retry runs on 27 too. Long transcripts: parts are sized by the model's window, `TranscriptTitler.chunkCharacters(contextSize:)` = (window − 1,300 reserved tokens for instructions, schema, answer and template) × 3.5 characters per token (below the measured 3.8-4.5): 9,786 characters for iOS 26's 4,096 tokens, 24,122 for iOS 27's 8,192 (`SystemLanguageModel.contextSize`, back-deployed; 8,192 measured on macOS 27), 65,450 for Gemma's 20,000 (`LocalLanguageModel.contextTokens`: about an hour of conversation in one request). Longer transcripts are summarized part by part as plain text, then title and summary are written from the numbered part summaries (repeated if those do not fit either). A request that still overflows (`TranscriptTitler.isContextExceeded`: iOS 26 `exceededContextWindowSize`, iOS 27 `contextSizeExceeded`) is tried again with parts 60 % as long, up to three times. Before version 2 the parts were a fixed 7,000 characters and the text had no speaker names. Code cleans the title (quotes, "Titel:", trailing period, openings like "Meeting über"/"Besprechung zum") and keeps it only with at most 6 words.

- `Recording.summary` stores the summary (also matched by search). The transcript shows it under the date line (secondary text, three lines; a longer one shows "More" and the whole summary is the button that opens and closes it; VoiceOver reads all of it), with search hits highlighted, so a transcript found only by its summary shows why.
- `Recording.summaryVersion` marks it done once a model answered, refused or failed for good, so a transcript is described once. Without a model (Apple Intelligence off, older iPhone, unsupported language) nothing changes, silently, and the next launch tries again. Likewise when the model can't answer right now (`TranscriptTitler.isTransient`: rate limited, which Apple documents for apps running in the background, busy, model assets missing or updating, time-out, cancelled): nothing is marked and the next launch or return to the foreground tries again; a part summary failing that way stops the whole description, so a title is never written from some parts only. Refusals, too long input and unknown errors are final.
- The title replaces the name only when `Recording.nameIsUserSet` is false (any edit in the transcript card's title field sets it) and the name is still one the app gave ("New Transcript N", "New Recording N", "Untitled Transcript") or the title a model gave it before (`Recording.automaticTitle`, kept since version 2: a better model may replace its own title), checked after the model answered, so names typed before this version or during the wait are never overwritten. Titles written before `automaticTitle` existed can't be told apart from typed names and stay.
- Versions: `TranscriptTitler.version` 2 (Apple's model), `localVersion` 100 (Gemma). `SpeakerIdentifier.summaryVersion` is the one transcripts should have (100 while Better Summaries is on), so a newer version or turning Gemma on describes every transcript again through the sweep, and turning it off keeps Gemma's descriptions (`max` of the two).
- Older transcripts are described once by the launch/foreground sweep (`prepareAndRetryPending`, newest first, behind any vocabulary passes). A recording stopped meanwhile goes first (it waits for at most the transcript being described), and the sweep starts nothing new while a recording is being made.

Evaluated on macOS 27 with the app file (scratch harness `titlebench`): 12 DiarBench meetings (reference words; read audiobook fragments cut into turns, so there is no real topic), 2 long DiarBench meetings (map-reduce), 40 real clips (20 MINDS-14 bank calls, 20 MLS audiobook excerpts), plus 4 hand-written realistic meetings. Every one got a summary; 55 of 56 benchmark items got a title (one 7-word title was rejected). Typical results: "Testphase Kreditprojekt und Abnahme" / "Kritischer Fehler im Scoring muss vor der Abnahme behoben werden; Abnahme am fünfzehnten geplant"; a 18,000-character steering meeting "Kreditprojekt Testphase und Budget" (its third topic, permissions, appears only in the summary); bank calls "Gemeinschaftskonto in der App", "Automatenlimit und Geldabhebung", "Firmenkredit und Möglichkeiten". Call titles and summaries were faithful; misheard words are carried over ("Kontostand und Wiener"). On the incoherent audiobook collages the model sometimes joins unrelated fragments ("Verlobung zwischen Prinzessinnen und Reiter"), interprets ("manipulative Beziehung") or adds a word ("König Krone" from "Würde Krone"), and some titles read like keyword lists ("Bild Auge"). About 1-2 s per short transcript, 8-15 s for 8,000-18,000 characters on an M5 Mac; not measured on an iPhone. After the error handling changed (refusals on 27, retries), the 4 realistic meetings got the same titles and summaries again.

### Better Summaries (Gemma 4 E2B)

Settings → After Recording → Summaries (the main page shows the model in use as the row's value); in DEBUG builds a Developer section's "Local Model" picker chooses the model, Gemma 4 E2B (the app's), E4B or Qwen3.5 4B (`LocalLanguageModel.Variant`; the other two to compare, not measured on a device: E4B is 5.1 GB of weights on an 8 GB iPhone at about half the speed, Qwen3.5 4B wrote the most complete summaries in the bench below at about 4× E2B's time; `BetterSummaries.variant`, each model with its own download state and folder, switching frees the other's weights), and "Download Now (3.6 GB)" (E4B: 5.1 GB, Qwen: 3.1 GB) fetches `mlx-community/gemma-4-e2b-it-4bit`, `…-e4b-it-4bit` or `mlx-community/Qwen3.5-4B-4bit` at a pinned revision (`Variant.repository`/`revision`/`files`) from Hugging Face into Application Support/Models (excluded from backups), file by file with progress (`BetterSummaries.download`; a complete file from an interrupted download is kept; keep the app open). Once there, the "Better Summaries" toggle (on by default) makes `SpeakerIdentifier.describe` use Gemma through `BetterSummaries.run`, and the Smart Corrections toggle becomes available (`applyCorrections`, the same `run`); "Delete Gemma" removes the files (summaries stay). `LocalLanguageModel` (an actor) loads the weights with mlx-swift-lm (`loadModelContainer(from:using:)` with a swift-transformers tokenizer bridge, the vision and audio towers are dropped at load) and generates greedily (temperature 0, thinking off, at most 400 tokens; the corrections sample at 0.25 with up to 3,000 tokens) with the instructions as the system turn; `TranscriptTitler.localTitleInstructions` asks for three plain lines ("Topics: …", "Summary: …", "Title: …", parsed by `parseLabelled`) with explicit prohibitions ("fasst zusammen", "Zusammenfassung der …") and repeats the task after the transcript (`localReminder`: without it Gemma went on writing the transcript).

- **Not while recording**: Gemma never runs while a recording is made (`BetterSummaries.isRecording`, set by `setRecording`): a description that would start then, or is stopped by it, waits in `SpeakerIdentifier.waitingForGemma` and runs right after Stop.
- **Foreground only**: iOS does not let apps use the GPU in the background. `run` throws `RetryLater` unless the app is active and cancels the request when it resigns active (`willResignActiveNotification`, before the GPU is taken away); the transcript stays undescribed and the next return to the foreground tries again.
- **Memory**: the weights are freed a minute after the last request, when a recording starts (`setRecording`) and when the app enters the background. A load that an unload overtook drops its result (`LocalLanguageModel.generation`).
- **Download**: a delegate-based `URLSession` per file reports bytes as they arrive (the async `download(from:delegate:)` gave no progress to a task delegate); the idle timer is off meanwhile, since auto-lock would suspend the app and fail the transfer. When the model becomes usable (downloaded, or the toggle turned on) `BetterSummaries.onBecameActive` starts the re-describe sweep, wherever the user is. Peak 3.7 GB on a Mac for an 8,300-token transcript.
- **Failures**: Gemma failing for another reason than being stopped (damaged files) uses Apple's model for that transcript, deletes the files and shows "Download failed" with Try Again, so not every launch retries it.
- **Simulator**: no MLX GPU there: Settings shows Apple Intelligence and "Better Summaries need an iPhone".

## Speaker identification after Stop

Turns come from **NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Diarization** (FluidAudio `Nemotron3Diarizer`, preset `c128-split-w8a8`: 98 MB + 2 MB, W8A8, Neural Engine graph, up to 8 speakers, 10 ms frames; `NemotronDiarizer`). The saved file is streamed through it in 10 s blocks (bit-identical to FluidAudio's whole-file `processComplete` on all 80 benchmark recordings, so a long recording is never held in memory whole). Each 10 ms frame goes to the most probable speaker slot when that probability is > 0.5, runs of one slot with gaps up to 0.1 s are joined, and a slot with less than 2 s in the whole recording is dropped (removes phantom speakers). `DiarizerSelector` uses pyannote (`FluidAudioDiarizer`, with `minSegmentDurationSeconds` 0.3, segmentation `stepRatio` 0.1 and VBx `warmStartFa` 0.1) while the Nemotron model is not downloaded, or when it fails to load or run. Nemotron has no speaker-count setting, so "Speakers: n" regroups its turns by voice (`SpeakerClustering`): unchanged when n is the count it found, whole speakers merged by average-linkage AHC when n is lower, and the speaker whose pieces split into the least alike halves (2-means) split when n is higher; pyannote takes n itself.

Measured with DiarBench (36 synthetic German meetings of 2-4 people from Multilingual LibriSpeech voices with normal, fast and overlapping turn-taking, each clean and in a simulated room, 72 recordings; Parakeet Ultra words; the app's own code: `DiarizerSelector`, final alignment, voices), on an M5 Mac. WDER = words attributed to the wrong speaker; DER with a 0.25 s collar:

| Post-stop path | WDER all | normal | fast | overlap | DER | right speaker count |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before (pyannote + live count + live fallback) | 16.9 % | 9.7 % | 29.5 % | 12.3 % | 18.0 % | 49/72 |
| **Nemotron** (automatic) | **1.77 %** | 0.46 % | 3.00 % | 1.94 % | 1.16 % | 70/72 |
| Nemotron, "Speakers: n" with the true n | 1.68 % | 0.20 % | 3.00 % | 1.94 % | 1.07 % | 72/72 |
| pyannote fallback (automatic) | 15.8 % | 11.0 % | 28.1 % | 9.3 % | 15.9 % | 46/72 |

## People and voice fingerprints

Naming a speaker in a transcript links it to a **Person** of that name (`Models/Person.swift`, a SwiftData model, created on first use) and stores that speaker's voice embedding as a sample; the person's fingerprint is the normalized mean of their samples. After each diarization, `PeopleDirectory.recognize` gives unnamed speakers the name of a person whose fingerprint matches (cosine similarity ≥ 0.68 for Nemotron's speakers, ≥ 0.75 for pyannote's and for transcripts from before Feder, with a 0.05 lead over the runner-up, one-to-one per transcript). Naming someone also recognizes them in older transcripts where their speaker is still unnamed. Recognition never adds samples; only names the user types do, so a wrong match cannot drift a fingerprint, and renaming a wrongly recognized speaker moves that speaker's sample to the right person.

**Renaming** (the sheet from a name row, `Views/SpeakerNameSheet.swift`): the name field is focused and prefilled with the current name, selected, so typing replaces it; Save (✓, or Return) names the speaker, a blank name or "Use Default Name" goes back to "Speaker N", Cancel (✕) or swiping the sheet down changes nothing. Below it the known people as large rows: the person this speaker is linked to (checkmark), then people whose fingerprint is like this speaker's voice ("Similar voice", cosine ≥ 0.60, `VoiceMatch.suggestion`: only an ordering hint, never applied by itself), then everyone else by name with the number of their transcripts ("Already in this transcript" for another speaker's person). Typing filters them as search suggestions match (name prefix, word prefix, then substring from 3 letters), the typed part bold (`SpeakerNameChoices`). Choosing a person names the speaker with that person's name, which also confirms a recognized speaker: its voice becomes one of the person's samples. The sheet replaces the earlier inline field, which was easy to miss (see UI tests) and lost its focus.

The voices come from what the transcript finally shows, whichever diarizer or speaker count produced it: `VoiceWindows` takes each speaker's runs of words (no pause over 1 s, at least 1 s, at most 10 s per window, longest first up to 180 s), and `VoiceEmbedder` embeds each window with pyannote's WeSpeaker models (256 values), the audio repeated to fill the model's 10 s input (zero padding shifts the FBank features, which are mean-normalized over the whole input, and makes different speakers alike) with frame weights on the words; a speaker's voice is the speech-weighted mean of its windows, and when 2-means splits them into two clearly different groups only the larger one counts. They are stored per transcript in `Recording.speakerVoices` (`voicesVersion` 1), with `Recording.speakerPeople` mapping speaker numbers to people. On the first launch with this version, `SpeakerIdentifier.refreshVoices` recomputes the voices of every finished transcript from the labels it shows, replaces its samples in people's fingerprints (`PeopleDirectory.replaceVoices`), gives a sample to people the user named where the transcript had no voice for that speaker (the old live fallback stored none), and runs recognition everywhere again. A named speaker the shown labels give no window (none of its runs of words lasts 1 s: someone who only said "Ja." or spoke in fast exchanges) keeps its sample and its old voice, which is in the same embedding space (cosine p50 0.98 with the new voice of the same speaker), so a person named only there stays recognizable and renaming finds the voice again; unnamed speakers without a new voice lose their old one. The migration takes one transcript at a time behind any recording stopped meanwhile and pauses while recording; the transcripts people's samples come from go first, and a recording finished before they are done gets its known names right after them, from the migrated fingerprints. Choosing a speaker count in the transcript card's Speakers menu resets that transcript's links and samples.

Calibration (`VoiceMatch` in `Models/Person.swift`), on the app's own post-stop path in the DiarBench harness (Nemotron turns, final alignment, VoiceWindows/VoiceEmbedder voices) and the voicerec protocol: 36 + 126 synthetic German meetings of 2-4 people from 30 real voices, clean and in a simulated room; meetings in order, each person named once at their first appearance and recognized in up to 12 later meetings; 5 rotating holdout groups of 6 never-named people × 4 enroll → test acoustic pairings (1,248 + 5,568 tests of named people, 912 + 1,992 of strangers, up to 24 known people). The operating point is the threshold with no more than 1 % of strangers named on both sets, with a cushion:

| Threshold / lead | Named correctly (36 / 126) | Someone else's name | Strangers named | Shown names right |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.60 / 0.05 | 97.8 / 96.3 % | 0 % | 0.9 / 0.6 % | 99.3 / 99.8 % |
| 0.65 / 0.05 | 97.8 / 95.2 % | 0 % | 0.4 / 0.1 % | 99.7 / 100 % |
| **0.68 / 0.05** (app) | **96.2 / 94.5 %** | 0 % | **0 / 0 %** | 100 / 100 % |
| 0.72 / 0.05 | 93.3 / 92.5 % | 0 % | 0 / 0 % | 100 / 100 % |

A lead of 0.03 instead of 0.05 changed nothing. If the user also names every miss: 96.8 / 98.5 % correct, still no stranger named (at 0.60 1.0 / 1.2 %). 0.68 rather than 0.60-0.66 because strangers were still named at 0.66, the benchmark is read speech (real meetings give less pure speakers), and more saved people give a stranger more chances: the per-pair false-accept rate at 0.68 is 0.0095 %, extrapolated (not measured) 0.24 % strangers named with 25 known people and 0.5 % with 50 (at 0.60: 2.8 % and 5.5 %). With the pyannote fallback (until Nemotron is downloaded) 0.68 gave 82.4 / 75.6 % correct, 0.7 / 1.3 % someone else's name and 2.1 / 3.4 % strangers named (merged and mixed speakers; it reaches no 1 % point at any threshold up to 0.80 on the 36 meetings), so its speakers are recognized at 0.75 (`VoiceMatch.fallbackThreshold`; `Recording.diarizerRaw` records which model made a transcript's speakers, and transcripts from before Feder, made with the old pyannote pipeline or live labels, count as pyannote's): 74.1 / 65.5 % correct, 0.7 / 0.8 % someone else's name, 1.6 / 0.9 % strangers named (same sweep and protocol). Before (pyannote centroids, live count and fallback, 0.6): 62 / 54 % correct, 2.8 / 4.9 % strangers named. Old fingerprints: the old pyannote centroids are close to the new voices of the same speaker (cosine p50 0.97), but 16 % of named people had no sample at all (live fallback); after `refreshVoices` (new voices from the old stored labels) people enrolled in old transcripts are recognized in new Nemotron transcripts at 94.2 / 92.7 % (0 / 0.6 % strangers named), against 78.9 / 75.9 % with the old samples kept.

Settings → People lists everyone, searchable, swipe to delete; a person shows the transcripts they speak in. Deleting a person removes the fingerprint and links; names already written in transcripts stay. Deleting a transcript removes its samples (and people left with neither samples nor transcripts). The Transcripts search also matches speaker names.

## Search

The Search tab (see Look) shows our search bar at the top (prompt "People, tags or words"); its close button goes back to the previous tab, and the text and tokens stay until changed. **Pull to search**: pulling the Transcripts list down past its top by 100 pt (rubber-banded: about 200 pt of finger travel) and lifting the finger there opens the Search tab (only on the Transcripts tab's plain list; also while a recording runs minimized in the accessory), with a light haptic once lifting will open it; pushing back up below the threshold before lifting, or a bounce after a fling, does nothing. The moment the finger lifts comes from `ScrollDragObserver` (`Views/ScrollDragObserver.swift`), which adds a target to the List's own `UIScrollView.panGestureRecognizer` (scrolling, taps and swipe actions are untouched): SwiftUI's `onScrollPhaseChange` stays `.idle` for a List that fits on screen (measured on iOS 27). (The progress ring around the former top-right Search button went with that button.) **Debounce**: what is typed is searched 0.3 s after the last keystroke (`RecordingsListView.typingPause`), and results, suggestions, the "n Transcripts" header and highlights all follow that text, so the list doesn't churn per keystroke; clearing the field, choosing a suggestion and adding or removing tokens apply at once. What is typed is matched in titles, places, tags, speaker names, summaries and the transcripts' words; every word must match somewhere (AND). Matching ignores case, accents and character width ("muller" finds Müller, "strasse" finds Straße); words under 3 characters match only at word starts ("an" finds "Anna", not "Stand"), longer ones also inside words (German compounds: "vertrag" finds "Arbeitsvertrag"); `#bud` typed as text matches tags only.

- **Suggestions**: while searching, the first list section (our own, not `.searchSuggestions`, which hides the results while typing) shows up to 8 people and tags as compact chips that wrap into lines (`WrappingLayout`; icon, name with the matched part bold, number of transcripts) matching the last typed word or the whole text (equal, prefix, word prefix inside the name, then substring from 3 characters), the matched part bold, with the number of transcripts (carried by each `Suggestion`); with an empty field the most used people and tags, half of each (the rest from the other kind when one has fewer); tokens already chosen are left out, and the suggestions stay after one is chosen. `#` suggests only tags, `@` only people. Choosing one replaces the typed fragment with a **token** (person: a named speaker must equal it; tag: the transcript must have it). A tag chip's tap on a row adds a tag token too.
- **Results** (newest first, "n Transcripts" header): the title with its hits highlighted, the matched people as filled chips, the tags (matched ones filled), and a two-line excerpt of the paragraph with the most search words, around its first hit (word-start hits preferred), with its speaker's name; a person token alone shows the first thing that person said; a hit only in the summary shows that part of the summary. Highlights use the accent colour. Without a query the list is the plain one.
- **Opened from a result** the transcript highlights the search words (in its summary under the date line too) and the matched speakers' names, starts at the result's paragraph (marked with a light accent background) and steps through the paragraphs with hits from the card's bottom toolbar ("3 of 12" with previous/next, which wrap around, between a More menu and Delete; see Transcript card). While the navigator is there, Share, Add Tag and Speakers share that More menu: seven items fit no iPhone's bar (iOS moved Next Match into an overflow menu on the iPhone 17, the iPhone Air and the iPhone 18 Pro Max, iOS 27 simulators; before Add Tag six fit from 402 pt on); with only a person token it steps through that person's turns.
- **Index** (`TranscriptSearchModel`): nothing is indexed before search is first presented. Then each recording becomes a `SearchDocument` (its paragraphs lowercased and accent-folded once into one UTF-8 buffer that `memmem` scans); afterwards only recordings whose `Recording.searchKey` changed are rebuilt. The key hashes name, tags, place, summary, speaker names, speaker status, `wordsRefined`, `vocabularyPending` and the speaker count, which change whenever the segments are rewritten, so the large `segments` are not read to detect changes (a new writer of segments must change one of them too). Reading the segments happens on the main actor (SwiftData); folding and every query run off it (`@concurrent`), 80 ms after the last change of the searched text (which itself trails typing by 0.3 s), a newer query cancelling the older one. Highlight ranges are found again in the original text, since folding can change lengths (ß → ss).
- Measured on an M5 Mac (500 transcripts × 10,000 words, 30.8 MB; search lab benchmark): index 0.5 ms per transcript (252 ms for 500), queries 15-40 ms (78 ms for two words), 10× faster than `localizedStandardContains` on a miss. Not measured: an iPhone, and how long SwiftData takes to load the segments of 500 long transcripts when search is first opened.

## Microphone

Recording always uses the iPhone's built-in microphone, even with AirPods or another headset connected. Bluetooth headset microphones run at telephony quality (24 kHz) and, in testing on device, produced audio the transcriber returned no text for.

## First-launch downloads

Processing is fully on-device; only the models are fetched once (all FluidAudio models are excluded from iCloud/iTunes backup):

- **Apple speech asset** for the current locale (`AssetInventory.assetInstallationRequest`), downloaded by the system the first time you record; the Record screen shows the progress. The language is the first entry of the iPhone's Settings ▸ General ▸ Language & Region list (`Locale.preferredLanguages`), not the app's own locale, so a German phone transcribes German.
- **FluidAudio diarization models** (~21 MB, pyannote community-1 + VBx CoreML models) downloaded from Hugging Face into `Application Support/FluidAudio/Models` the first time speakers are identified. Always needed: their WeSpeaker models make every speaker's voice, and they diarize until Nemotron is there. This never blocks recording: offline, recordings stay `pending` with an explanatory row and are retried automatically the next time the app comes to the foreground (or via "Try Again").
- **NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Diarization** (~100 MB, `nemotron-3-diarization/split/Nemotron3Diarizer_c128_split_w8a8.mlmodelc` + two small `.bin` files in the same folder; `NemotronModel`). Downloaded automatically at launch and on every return to the foreground, but only on Wi-Fi (not on cellular, hotspot or Low Data Mode); "Download Now" under Settings ▸ Speaker Identification downloads on any network and shows progress ("Model: NVIDIA Nemotron" once it is there). Until then, or if it fails to load, speakers are identified with pyannote. FluidAudio discards and re-downloads it when its weights version changes. Not downloaded on the simulator.
- **FluidAudio LS-EEND live model** (~45 MB, `ls-eend/dih3/optimized/dih3/500ms/ls_eend_dih3_500ms.mlmodelc` in the same folder) downloaded in the background at launch, on every return to the foreground and when "Live Speaker Labels" is switched on, while the setting is on. It never blocks recording: a recording started before the model is loaded simply has no live labels. A failed or interrupted download is deleted and retried on the next foreground.

- **Parakeet Ultra** (~610 MB, `parakeet-ultra` in the same folder) for the full-accuracy pass, while "Parakeet" is the engine in Settings (default). Downloaded automatically at launch and on every return to the foreground, but only on Wi-Fi (not on cellular, hotspot or Low Data Mode); "Download Now" in Settings downloads on any network and shows progress. Until it is there, Apple's recognizer does the pass. Not downloaded on the simulator, which uses fake engines.

## Live latency notes

- Live text: `SpeechTranscriber` is created with the `.timeIndexedProgressiveTranscription` preset (`.volatileResults` + `.fastResults` + `.audioTimeRange`). Without `.fastResults` the model returns nothing until about 11.6 s of audio are buffered, then one burst; with it, a volatile update arrives about every second (measured on a Mac with German speech: display lag median 4.7 s → 0.16 s, worst 11 s → about 1 s). `.fastResults` also produces the saved final words, slightly less accurately (one German test clip: 4.8 % → 6.0 % word error rate).
- The speech model is loaded in `prepare()` (`prepareToAnalyze`), so the "Preparing…" overlay covers it and the first audio is not queued behind it.
- Live labels run on a `.utility` worker with a 500 ms step (two CoreML calls per second, CPU only) so speech recognition keeps priority, and publish a snapshot after every step (0.5 s; it was 1 s). Debug builds log how far the newest recognized word of each transcription result trails the microphone ("text … s behind" in the Xcode console); expect about 1 s or less on device while someone is speaking. The "input" figure only grows when the iPhone's compute cannot keep up with the audio.
- Speaker labels: SpeechTranscriber finalizes only about every 15 s of speech (words final p50 8.7 s after they were spoken), and its provisional text arrives as one run. Labelling only final words left a new speaker's first words under the previous speaker for seconds. Replayed on 24 benchmark conversations (48 min, 876 speaker changes; the latency study's harness running the app's own `LiveTranscript` and `LiveTranscriptModel`, which reproduce its best configuration without a new model download exactly), from before to now: the right speaker shows p50 5.5 → 2.2 s and p90 16.9 → 3.1 s after a change (fast exchanges 4.7 / 15.8 → 2.0 / 2.9 s, normal 9.1 / 18.6 → 2.4 / 3.7 s); words ever shown under a wrong speaker 59 → 44 %, share of display time under a wrong speaker 30.5 → 26.9 %, label flips 62 → 50 per minute, words right at Stop 77.1 → 77.5 % (that is LS-EEND's accuracy, not latency). A 200 ms LS-EEND step (another 43 MB model) would gain about 0.16 s more. A name that had shown used to go away for a moment and come back, mostly at a new speaker's first words and after a pause (reported on the iPhone). The cause: a name depended on some word of its paragraph being confirmed at that moment, and every hand-over lost the confirmation for `hold` — the final result (a finalized word started its hold again), the final removing SpeechTranscriber's whole provisional run (the next speaker's words with it, until the next volatile result), and every re-timing of the provisional words (the hold was kept per 0.1 s frame of an estimated time). Now: provisional words have ids that last across volatile results (`VolatileTimer` aligns each result with the last by edit distance) and into their final version, holds are kept per word (`WordSteady`, `SteadyLabels.carry`, not taken over where the final word's own label disagrees), the run's words after the finalized ones stay, and a name once shown stays while its paragraph keeps the same first word under the same speaker (`LiveNames`). Words are still placed by their newest label, as before: keeping a held label until another one had held too (tried) held words under the previous speaker when LS-EEND's labels went back and forth, and carrying estimated times across a final (in the build of 2026-10-05 16:32) put the next speaker's first words into the pause before them; both are gone, and each run is timed from its own start again. Measured by replaying real SpeechTranscriber and LS-EEND output (scratch bench in `build/qa/livebench`: three two-minute German meetings spoken by macOS's Anna and Reed, streamed in real time through the app's SpeechTranscriber preset and LS-EEND configuration, the events replayed through `LiveTranscript` and a port of `LiveTranscriptModel`'s paragraphs, scored against the script): names shown, hidden and shown again 34 → 1 times in 31 speaker changes, words under a wrong visible speaker (a paragraph without a name reads as the named one above) 2.8 → 2.4 % of their screen time, the right name p50 2.3 s after a change as before. Two clearly different synthetic voices are easier for LS-EEND than two colleagues, so the live labels' accuracy on real conversations is not measured by this.
- Text on screen: words appear p50 0.51 s, p90 1.37 s after they were spoken (SpeechTranscriber's pace). They used to fade in from transparent, blurred and lowered over 0.45 s, staggered up to 0.4 s more, so they reached 55 % opacity p50 0.75 s and p90 1.61 s after they were spoken (fully opaque p50 0.96 s), well after the console had printed them. Now they are drawn at 60 % opacity, unblurred and in place, as soon as they appear (readable p50 0.51 s, p90 1.37 s; fully opaque p50 0.66 s). From a result arriving to its text being drawn takes p50 4.3 ms, p90 5.2 ms in the simulator (Debug build; `-liveTrace`), and p50 5.6 ms, p90 6.6 ms with a 30-minute transcript.
- Main-actor cost per live update (labels and paragraphs incremental since this version): on an M5 Mac (release build, synthetic speech, `LiveLat mainactor`) 0.003-0.014 ms for the transcript state plus 0.015 ms for `LiveTranscriptModel.update` at 30 and 60 minutes, against 1-2 ms for the old full re-alignment on every snapshot; Debug build 0.02-0.3 ms. In the simulator (Debug, 30-minute transcript, about 4,800 words in 430 paragraphs): RecordingSession p50 0.31 ms, p90 1.1 ms (the first labelling of the whole backlog once took 8 ms), `LiveTranscriptModel.update` p50 0.33 ms, p90 0.52 ms. Not measured on an iPhone.
