# Feder

Feder (formerly Scribe; Xcode target `Recorder`, product, module and executable `Feder`, bundle id `de.timtody.feder`, published under timtody; until 2026-10-08 the bundle id was `de.cofinpro.recorder` and the product `Recorder`, and transcripts made under that identity stay in that app) is a local-only iPhone app (iOS 26+) that records audio, transcribes it live on device, shows preliminary speaker labels while recording, identifies speakers accurately after the recording ends, and lets you rename speakers, tag recordings and search them. Nothing leaves the phone: audio, transcripts and speaker data stay in the app's Documents/Application Support folders.

`PIPELINE.md` is the map of what happens to a recording (both stages, every model, the measurements, the ideas on the table and what was tried and dropped); `TASKS.md` the open work; `APPSTORE.md` the checklist to the App Store (free app, tips via In-App Purchase). This README is the detailed reference.

## What it does

- **Record**: tap the red button, watch the live transcript (Apple `SpeechAnalyzer`/`SpeechTranscriber`), tap Stop. The provisional text follows in a lighter colour and firms up in place; new words are readable the moment they appear (drawn at 60 % opacity, fully opaque within 0.2 s); a small level meter beside the big timer above the Stop follows the microphone; haptics mark the presses on Transcribe and Stop. The transcript follows new text while you are at the bottom; scroll up to reread and it stops following until you tap "Jump to Live".
- **Speakers**: while recording, FluidAudio's streaming LS-EEND model labels the live transcript, the provisional words included: a new speaker starts a new paragraph at once, and the speaker's coloured name appears above it once the label has held for half a second. After Stop the WAV is diarized again on device with NVIDIA Nemotron 3 (via FluidAudio; with FluidAudio's pyannote pipeline until its 100 MB model is downloaded), which replaces the live labels (the offline result is the source of truth; the transcript card shows "Refining speakers…" meanwhile, also while it is queued right after Stop: "Try Again" only shows for a pending transcript that is not queued, or when the speaker model could not be loaded). Speakers whose voice matches a known person get that person's name. The transcript card opens each speaker turn with a name row: the speaker's name and the turn's start time (grey while preliminary). Once speakers are final, tap a name (the button is the name and its time plus 16 pt, at least 56 pt tall including the space above the name; it highlights and gives a light haptic; until October 2026 it was the full width, so a tap at the right edge renamed too and the naming tip pointed at the middle of the screen) or long-press it → "Rename Speaker…": a sheet names that speaker in every turn of the transcript, by typing or by choosing one of the known people (see People and voice fingerprints). While speakers are still being identified, a tap shows a short notice under the name instead. Long-pressing the transcript's text keeps the system's text selection (iOS 27: words and ranges with Copy, Look Up, Translate; iOS 26: the whole paragraph with Copy, Share). Live labels can be switched off in Settings (Live Speaker Labels, on by default).
- **Languages**: English and German (`Recorder/Localizable.xcstrings`, `Recorder/InfoPlist.xcstrings` for the microphone, location and Face ID prompts); the app follows the iPhone's language, English otherwise. Source strings are English in code: literals in `Text`/`Button`/`Label` are extracted by the compiler, strings built at runtime (status line, Settings values, errors, the default speaker name) use `String(localized:)`. Dates follow the app's language too (`AppLanguage`: "Yesterday at 16:56", "Gestern um 16:56"; within a week the weekday alone in German), and so do the language names in the record accessory. Stored default names are written in the language of the moment ("New Transcript 3", "Neues Transkript 3"), so `TranscriptTitler.isDefaultName` accepts both; the default speaker name "Speaker 1"/"Sprecher 1" is never stored. The transcripts' own words and Gemma's German summary prompts are not translated. UI tests run in English (`launch` passes `-AppleLanguages (en)`); `SettingsTests.testGermanLocalization` checks the German labels.
- **Look**: Liquid Glass. The Transcripts list is laid out like Voice Memos: a plain list on the screen's own background (white, black in dark mode), compact two-line rows (title; then date · duration · place and as many people/tag chips as fit, "+n" for the rest, laid out by `MetaLineLayout`: everything; else without the place; else fewer chips with "+n"; at last no chips and the place truncated. Not `ViewThatFits`: iOS 26 measures its candidates on SwiftUI's async render thread, where their main-actor `ForEach` closures trapped in Swift 6's isolation check; the layout is `nonisolated` and captures nothing. Titles are one line), the large "Transcripts" heading as the list's first row (not a navigation bar title: it scrolls away with the list; the navigation bar is hidden), and in the Search tab the suggestions and results start right under the search bar ("Suggested" and "n Transcripts" are caption rows, `listCaption`, not section headers, which a plain list places ~22 pt lower). Swiping a row left shows Voice Memos' wide pills with the title under them: Delete (outermost; a full swipe deletes, without a confirmation) and Share (the plain-text transcript; `ShareSheet` presents the system share sheet (the activity view controller itself, with its own fast presentation) at once, since `ShareLink` prepared its items first; a still image over the row's snap-back and a container sheet were tried and dropped, as they made the sheet appear later). These are List's own `swipeActions`; iOS picks their look by the row's height (measured on iOS 27, iPhone 17): up to 59 pt capsules with the title inside, about 62-70 pt wide pills with the title under them, from 76 pt round buttons (`UIContextualAction` has no public style for it), so rows are two lines with a minimum height of 62 pt (`minRowHeight`, set on the row content so the caption rows stay small): the pill's height follows the row's (62 pt rows: 60 × 34 pt pills, 66 pt: 60 × 38), and Voice Memos' rows are about 62 pt with 60 × 33 pt pills (measured on a screenshot of it). Rows with a search snippet are taller and get the round buttons. The row has no background of its own, so a swiped row shows no edge. **Tabs** (`Views/MainView.swift`, Apple Music's Liquid Glass layout, iOS 26): a `TabView` with Settings (gear), Transcripts (waveform) and Search (magnifier, at the right end) in one tab bar, and the record control as the tab bar's bottom accessory above it (`.tabViewBottomAccessory`, where Music has its mini player; the system draws the glass capsule). Scrolling down minimizes the bar to three items like Music's (`.tabBarMinimizeBehavior(.onScrollDown)`): the current tab, the accessory ("● Transcribe") and Search as its own circle on the right. On iOS 27 Search is a `Tab(role: .search)` all the time: expanded it sits in the bar like any tab, minimized it is that circle (checked frame by frame and by accessibility frames on the iPhone 17 simulator: the same layout in both states as the earlier switch). iOS 26 set a `.search` tab apart as the circle in both states, so there Search is an ordinary tab while the bar is expanded and takes the search role only while it is minimized (`isBarMinimized`, from the accessory's `tabViewBottomAccessoryPlacement == .inline`). On iOS 27 that switch replaced the Search tab (UITab ↔ UISearchTab, a new object each time, in the same update as the bar's morph), and on the iPhone the magnifier sometimes dropped into its place from above as the bar unfolded. Being last in the bar, Search stays at the right end instead of jumping there from the middle. `isBarMinimized` starts `true` and the accessory resets it on its first appearance, before the first frame: the Search circle then exists from launch. Created only at the first collapse, it came late to the minimized bar's layout, and the accessory was laid out as wide as without it, running under the circle for ~0.3 s before it snapped to its width (frame by frame on the iPhone 17 simulator; every later collapse was fine). The tab bar's size is the system's, the same as Music's (no size API). The "Transcripts" and "Settings" headings are not pinned: the first list row and the first section header, they scroll away with the content (no navigation bar titles), at the same place (x 20 pt like the list's row text, same top: the section header is pulled 11 pt left and 5 pt up); the Search tab has no heading. Leaving the Search tab puts the keyboard away (MainView resigns the first responder: the tab's view stays alive, so its focus state alone did not). A row's date line is one VoiceOver element with all of it (date, duration, place, people, tags) and a "Filter by tag" action per tag: `MetaLineLayout` parks what doesn't fit off-screen, where it stayed in the accessibility tree. **Transcript card** (`Views/TranscriptCard.swift`, Apple Music's "Now Playing"): a transcript is always shown in one full-screen card, the same surface for recording and for viewing, never pushed. The card is MainView's one `fullScreenCover` with a zoom transition (`.navigationTransition(.zoom)`; sources are marked with `.cardSource(id)`, `matchedTransitionSource` in MainView's namespace): out of the record accessory for a recording, out of the tapped row for a transcript from the Transcripts list, the Search results or Settings › People › person (all through the `openTranscript` environment action; the row ids are scoped by list, `OpenTranscriptAction.rowSource`, because every tab stays alive and the same transcript is a row in several lists at once). It covers the tab bar and the accessory. At the top left a glass back button (`CardCloseButton`, a 44 pt circle with a chevron, where a pushed page has its Back and where Photos puts it over a zoomed photo; VoiceOver "Minimize"): tapping it, or swiping the card to the right from the left screen edge (`CardSwipeToClose`: UIKit's own back gesture, a `UIScreenEdgePanGestureRecognizer`, so the card follows the finger and closes past a third of the width or a flick; scroll views, text selection and the tag chips' horizontal scroll give way to an edge pan as they do in every navigation stack) shrinks it back to where it came from: during a recording into the accessory (recording goes on), a transcript into its row. Swiping down only scrolls the content: the zoom transition's own interactive dismissal is off (`interactiveDismissDisabled`), because pulling the card down competed with scrolling the transcript (the earlier grabber). The content starts 60 pt down and scrolls under the button's glass (no band, no fade). No top bar: nothing at the top right. While recording the card shows `RecordView`, laid out like Voice Memos' recording sheet and exactly like the transcript it becomes: at the top the name the transcript will get ("New Transcript N", fixed when the session is made at Record, `RecordingSession.transcriptName`, so the title field never shows its "Title" placeholder while the card zooms in) as a title and its date line (the start time, then " · 📍 place" fading in once `PlaceLocator` has it, `RecordingSession.place`; none with Settings' Save Location off, and none while the first recording runs: it asks for location at its Stop, not over the live recording, so that transcript gets where it ended), same top offset, margin, fonts and positions as the transcript's (a disabled-looking but real `TextField`: a `Text` sat 1 pt higher); the live transcript below; at the bottom the level meter beside a big 44 pt timer, "<language> · On device" and the large Stop. While a model for better transcripts is still to come (the live speaker model, Nemotron, or Parakeet when it was asked for; not Gemma, which writes titles and summaries: `AppServices.awaitsTranscriptModels`), a line under the language says "Preliminary quality. It gets better once the models are downloaded." ("Vorläufige Qualität. Sie wird besser, sobald die Modelle heruntergeladen sind."); once shown it stays for that recording, so the timer and Stop don't move when a download ends midway. When Stop saves something the same card turns into the transcript in place (no push, no new page; `TranscriptCard.Turn`), starting at Stop, not when saving has finished (Stop → saved measured 120–180 ms on the simulator, 70 ms of it stopping the audio engine): title and date line stay where they are (the transcript's lie exactly over them; a pixel diff of the last recording frame and the first transcript frame differs only by antialiasing, ≤ 15 grey levels); the big timer shrinks and flies into the date line's duration slot while the place moves aside for it (0.45 s, the timer's text is the saved duration, so it lands on the transcript's digits; a place that fits beside the date alone but not beside date and duration moves down to the second line as the slot opens, `DateLineLayout`); the live text, meter and caption fade out (0.2 s); and the large Stop's Liquid Glass melts into the transcript's bottom toolbar. The melt: the system bar can't morph from a 72 pt disc (a bar item is at most a 48 pt glass; only a regular-glass capsule ≥ ~92 pt wide morphed into the bar's group, a round or `.glassProminent` Stop just faded, and Delete never split off), so two glass shapes in a `GlassEffectContainer` start on top of each other on the Stop (one blob) and spring apart onto the bar's measured frames (a 0.45 s spring with bounce 0.25 computed per frame and eased onto its end at exactly 0.55 s: SwiftUI's `.bouncy` with completion `.removed` only completed 1.0–1.4 s after Stop, so the bar looked finished for over half a second before its buttons worked; the real bar now takes over ~0.7 s after Stop) (Share + Add Tag + Edit Text + Speakers + Listen capsule 28, 798, 285 × 48, 227.67 wide before Listen, 175 before Edit Text and 116 before Add Tag; Edit Text's symbol greyed, as the real button is right after Stop, Listen's not: the card has found the audio by the hand-over; and Delete 326, 798, 48 × 48 on a 402 × 874 pt iPhone, from the system's glass and image views; another item in that group needs measuring again; 28 pt from the screen edges, 42 pt above the safe area's bottom), the red square shrinking away within 0.1 s (slower, it hung as a pink dot in the gap between the two shapes) and the bar's symbols (17 pt medium, large) fading in above the container (inside it they took on the glass's vibrancy). Then the real toolbar appears under them in one frame without animation, and they go in that frame: the transcript view was inserted with its bar hidden at least 50 ms before (shown in the same update that inserts its view, iOS materialised it: an empty frame, then ~230 ms of blur), and until then a 52 pt near-transparent bottom `safeAreaBar` stands in for the bar's soft scroll edge (`Color.clear` gave none). The replicas take no touches and are hidden from VoiceOver; users and tests reach only the real toolbar. Only where the bar was measured (iOS 27, Face ID iPhones, text below the accessibility sizes); elsewhere, and with Reduce Motion, the Stop fades out, the timer fades out while the duration fades in at its place, and the bar fades in. The timer's flight, the fades and the melt are drawn frame by frame (`TimelineView`), not with SwiftUI animations, on the turn's own clock (`TurnClock`): the frames' time from the first frame drawn after Stop, each step at most 1/30 s. Right after Stop the main thread is busy for ~0.1 s (saving, inserting the transcript), and an implicit fade only started after that; timed by the wall clock from Stop, the first frame seen was already a tenth of a second in (the live text half faded, the timer 24 pt up), a cut rather than a fade. On the turn's clock a stall only holds the motion up. The melt's glass also stands by over the Stop while recording (the same layer at 1 % opacity): inserted only at Stop, the render server needed ~0.1 s for its first frame. The transcript is often saved within 0.15 s, so the recording's title, date line and timer stay on top until the timer has landed and the melt has settled. The transcript's summary, tags and text fade in (0.25 s) as the live text has faded out, into the space it left (a crossfade showed live and saved paragraphs at once; starting it as the timer landed left the body empty for ~0.25 s). If the recording is discarded after all while turning, the melt fades and the card closes as it stands; a failure crossfades to its screen. A card opened while the recording is being saved (Stop, swiped down, "Saving…" tapped) turns too. The saved transcript carries what the card showed: `createdAt` is the recording's start, and the place is written before saving when it was found already (otherwise once it is). The live text is frozen at Stop (`RecordingSession.show`), so words finalized while saving don't re-split its paragraphs just before; when the speakers are refined later the transcript's paragraphs change in place, without an animation (see What happens after Stop). The transcript (`Views/RecordingDetailView.swift`): the title (editable), date · duration · place (`TranscriptDateLine`), summary, tags, speakers and the final transcript, and the system's bottom toolbar (`.bottomBar` toolbar items, Liquid Glass): Share Transcript (a menu of formats, see Share; text goes to the same `ShareSheet` as the list's Share swipe action, so both show the transcript's title and the app symbol in the sheet's header), Add Tag (inserts the tag line, see Organize), Edit Text (see Edit Text), Speakers (a menu with the speaker-count picker: Automatic, 2–6 Speakers) and Listen (headphones, see Listen) in one glass group, Delete Transcript (trash, asks first: the confirmation is attached to the toolbar button, so it comes out of it as in Photos and Notes; the card closes, then the transcript is deleted once the view has disappeared, since reading a deleted @Model traps) on the right; opened from a search result, "n of m" with Previous Match/Next Match as a group between them (see Search). With search hits a second flexible spacer made the bar too wide for an iPhone 17 (iOS moved Next and Delete into an overflow "…" menu), so Delete follows the hits after a fixed gap; while hits are shown, Share, Add Tag, Edit Text, Speakers and Listen share one "More" menu (ellipsis) on every iPhone, since seven items fit no iPhone's bar (see Search), so the navigator and Delete stay visible. A hit is scrolled to the centre only when it isn't wholly on screen (like Safari's Find on Page), so a result whose first hit is near the top opens with the title in place. The transcript runs on under the bottom toolbar with the system's soft scroll edge (`scrollEdgeEffectStyle(.soft, for: .bottom)`), as in Notes and Mail and as the live view has under its Stop. The back button floats over the top left of the transcript (an overlay; the content starts 60 pt lower, `contentMargins`, and scrolls under the glass). Not a top `safeAreaBar` with the system's scroll edge effect: on iOS 27 that switched off the paragraphs' text selection in the card (a long press selected nothing and showed no Copy; found by `testLongPressMenusKeepTextSelection`, and gone with the overlay). What the card shows (`TranscriptCardContent`: the session, the transcript, the search context, the zoom source) is an observable object the card reads itself: as plain state read only in the cover's content closure, the cover came up blank and zoomed back into the wrong source. It stays until the cover's `onDismiss`, so the card keeps its content and its zoom while it shrinks; only then a session that ended (saved, discarded, failed) is dropped. Stop in the accessory while the card is minimized opens the card on the saved transcript; a discarded recording closes the card. A request to open the card while it is still shrinking away (a row or Transcribe tapped, or the recording saved during the zoom back) waits until its `onDismiss` (`MainView.pendingCard`) and then opens it, so the dismissal neither wipes the new content nor zooms the old card into the new source. A recording saved while the card shows another transcript waits in the list, and the tab behind that card (its zoom source) stays as it is. The session is started at once on the tap on Transcribe: the audio session and the engine come up on the `AudioHardware` actor, never on the main thread (started there, the card only came up after "Preparing…", which a 120 ms delay used to paper over), and "Preparing…" is shown only once preparing has taken 0.4 s (the first load of the speech model); normally the card opens straight onto the empty transcript. The haptics come with the presses themselves (Transcribe: medium, Stop: light), not with the state changes a moment later.
The accessory (`Views/RecordAccessory.swift`, Music's mini player sizes and layout): without a recording a solid red dot, "Transcribe" with a second line under it (the language, "German · On device", as Music shows the artist under the song; "Preparing speaker model…" while Nemotron compiles after an update or after its download, Nemotron's download, "Downloading speaker model · 42 %" or, while it waits, one of three lines from NetworkPath: "Speaker model · Waiting for Wi-Fi" (cellular or a hotspot), "Speaker model · Offline" or "Speaker model · Low Data Mode"; before, offline read "Waiting for Wi-Fi" or ended in "Download Failed", and Low Data Mode read "Waiting for Wi-Fi" while the status bar showed Wi-Fi. Then Gemma's background download: "Downloading Gemma · 42 %", or "Gemma · Waiting for Wi-Fi", "Gemma · Offline" or "Gemma · Low Data Mode", so they are seen without opening Settings) and at the end one control, as Music has its forward button: the next recording's language ("DE ⌄", a menu of the phone's own languages and German, English, French, Spanish, Italian and Dutch; `TranscriptionLanguage`, stored only when it differs from the phone's language, so a change of the phone's language carries through). Before it every recording was transcribed in the phone's first language, so an English meeting on a German iPhone came out as German; the accessory's capsule is the system's, as wide as the tab bar, and the second line and the control fill the room the single word left empty. Minimized (inline) only the dot and "Transcribe"; while recording Stop (a red square) in the record dot's place, so the control stays under the finger, then the time over the language and, once `PlaceLocator` has it, the place ("German · Mitte, Berlin"), and the microphone level (`LevelMeter`) at the end (VoiceOver: "Recording" with the elapsed time as its value; a tap beside Stop opens the card); "Preparing…", "Saving…" and "Can't Record" in between. Every state puts its mark (the dot, 18 pt, Stop, the spinner) in one leading slot a full touch target wide (`markSlot`), so nothing jumps between them; before, Stop sat at the far right and the pulsing dot at the left. Like the mini player opening into "Now Playing", a tap opens the transcript card on the recording (`RecordView`: title and date line, live transcript, level, time and a large Stop, `StopButtonLabel`) out of the accessory with a zoom transition; swiping it to the right or its back button (Minimize) shrinks it back into the accessory and recording goes on (MainView owns the session: it starts it and handles Saved/Discarded); after Stop the card turns into the new transcript (see Transcript card), and the Transcripts tab is selected behind it, so the new transcript tops the list when the card closes. The accessory is always enabled (VoiceOver label "Transcribe", what it shows). 
Search (the Search tab: the same list in search mode, `RecordingsListView(isSearch: true)`) has our own bar (`Views/TranscriptSearchBar.swift`) at the top of the screen like Apple Music's, a top safe-area bar (the results scroll under it and fade out; scrolling them dismisses the keyboard at once, `.immediately`, in either direction): a glass capsule with the field (focused whenever the tab appears) and a clear button, the glass close button beside it (back to the tab Search was opened from), and the person/tag tokens floating under it as accent-tinted glass chips (`Views/SearchTokensBar.swift`) that wrap into more rows when they don't fit one, growing downward (`WrappingLayout`; a name wider than the whole row truncates in the middle; one `GlassEffectContainer`, chips morph in and out; a tap on a chip removes it). The text and tokens are shared by the tabs (`SearchState`): pulling the Transcripts list down past its top and letting go, or tapping a tag chip, opens the Search tab (with that tag; a tag chip clears text typed in an earlier search). "More" menus use the borderless `ellipsis`. Content (rows, tag chips, transcript) has no glass. Accent colour Cofinpro blue #006CFF (dark mode #3D8BFF for contrast), distinct from the red record control. Speaker names cycle through the system colours blue, orange, green, purple, pink and teal (`SpeakerPalette`). The app icon is `Recorder/AppIcon.icon` (Icon Composer: a white Liquid Glass feather on a clear blue gradient, navy in dark mode; one shape, the silhouette with its cut-ins and the shaft, as set in Icon Composer: combined lighting, specular, translucency 0.65, a layer-colour shadow 0.5, scaled 115 %. Two smaller copies of the body nested on it, like the stacked houses of Apple's Home icon, were tried, as inward offsets and as narrower feathers: the feather is too small for that effect. Assembled by the user in Icon Composer and tuned from there: with all three shapes in one group and combined lighting, the glass merged them into one flat piece; strong translucency (0.7) washed the quill out at 60 pt and a layer-colour shadow glowed in dark mode. Before it: a single white feather, a quill pen on paper, a pixel-art quill, a vector quill writing a sound wave, a quill on a blue-to-purple tile), a vector quill writing a sound wave that faded into a scribble on slate, and a quill on a blue-to-purple tile), a four-barb saw vane (torn sail), a leaf vane (leaf on a stick), a wave snapping into a straight line (no fade), a tail that was a smaller copy of the wave (quieter sound, not writing). - **Organize**: an automatic title and summary after Stop (Gemma 4 E2B on device, once downloaded in Settings → Recording → Summaries, on iPhones with 8 GB or more; without it there are none; the summary is folded into one "Summary" row under the date line of the transcript, opened by a tap; under its date line and summary every transcript says "AI-generated content can contain mistakes."), inline-editable title, tags (`TagEditor`: no tag line at all while a transcript has none; the bottom toolbar's Add Tag inserts it with a focused field after the pills, like Mail's recipients: a comma turns the word before it into a pill, pasted "a, b, c" makes three, Return adds the last word and ends, and so does leaving the field, which takes a line without tags away again; meanwhile the other transcripts' tags are suggested in a row under it, the most used first, then those matching what is typed, with the typed part in bold, the same ranking as the search's suggestions; pills keep their × to remove), search across titles, people, tags, summaries and the transcripts' words (see Search), swipe to delete, share the plain-text transcript.
- **Import** (`Services/AudioImporter.swift`): audio from elsewhere becomes a transcript. The record accessory's Import button (`square.and.arrow.down`) sits at its end, right of the language; minimized, Import stays on the right and the language goes (as Music keeps play there). It opens the system's file picker for audio (`fileImporter`, `public.audio`). Feder is also in other apps' share sheets for audio (Voice Memos, Files…: `CFBundleDocumentTypes` for `public.audio`, rank Alternate, `LSSupportsOpeningDocumentsInPlace` NO, so a copy arrives in Documents/Inbox and `onOpenURL` gets it; the copy is deleted once imported). The file is converted off the main actor into a recording's own format, a 16 kHz mono 16-bit WAV in AudioFileStore (AVAudioFile + a persistent AVAudioConverter in 32k-frame chunks; a read past the last frame throws eofErr, so the input block stops at the file's length; all channels are averaged into one first with vDSP: converting to mono, AVAudioConverter keeps only the first channel, which lost the guest on the right channel of a two-microphone interview, and its `downmix` doesn't mix discrete multichannel files), named after the file (Voice Memos' "Neue Aufnahme 3" counts as a default name and gets Gemma's title), dated by its creation-date metadata or the file's date, in the language it is spoken in (`Services/LanguageProbe.swift`: Apple's SpeechTranscriber takes one locale and has no language identification, so three 20 s windows of the file, at a tenth, the middle and four fifths of a long one, else its first minute, are transcribed in each candidate whose speech model is installed, the accessory's language and the iPhone's languages, at most three; a probe downloads nothing, a missing model of the chosen language still comes with the words pass. NLLanguageRecognizer reads each window's text: a model transcribing its own language writes text recognized as that language, a wrong one doesn't. Per window, of the candidates whose text is mostly their own language the most confident wins, else the language the text is in when Apple has it; the windows vote, a tie goes to the accessory's language (judged as one excerpt, a 30 s English intro outweighed a German meeting). Chinese counts by script (the recognizer says zh-Hans or zh-Hant), and Apple's variant of a language is its region's, else the likely one: `supportedLocale(equivalentTo:)` picked en_SG or en_GB for "en". Letters, not words, decide whether a window said enough: Chinese and Japanese text has no spaces. Mac harness `build/qa/langprobe` with the app's own file: 39 real German files (Multilingual LibriSpeech meetings and utterances, one with 36 s of English spliced in at 18 s) and 6 synthetic English ones right with either language chosen, a French one found through the text, Traditional and Simplified Chinese kept as chosen or as the iPhone's language; 0.2–1.7 s each on an M5; confidence alone was right too, but by a thin margin for English: the German model reached 0.84 on English speech against the English model's 0.95. Skipped on the simulator, which has no speech models). Then it goes through the steps after Stop from the words on (it has no words, so the full-accuracy pass writes them; an import's first words don't flash as changes), and the card opens on it out of the accessory, where the status line says what is being done ("Importing…" in the accessory meanwhile). Files that arrive while one is converting (shared one after another, or several at once) wait their turn, and the card opens on the last; a card in Edit Text stays open (closing it would drop its drafts) and the imports wait in the list. A file the transcriber finds no speech in says "No speech was found in this recording."; a words pass that failed (offline while the language's speech model must download, a language it doesn't know) leaves the import `.failed` with "Couldn't transcribe this recording…" and Try Again, instead of an empty transcript marked done (a recording keeps its live words then; an import has none). The original file isn't kept; nothing leaves the iPhone.
- **Listen** (`Services/TranscriptPlayer.swift`, `Views/PlayerBar.swift`, `Views/WordText.swift`): a mode, not always on (the user found a player and tappable words in every card too much in the face). The headphones at the end of the card's toolbar group (in the More menu with search hits; greyed out without the audio file, as for the seeded samples) show the player above the bottom toolbar: play or pause, the time, a slider, the time left and ✕, in a glass capsule (the card's bottom safe-area bar, so the transcript scrolls under it). ✕ (or the headphones again) stops playback and ends Listen; closing the card does too. ## Architecture

**Modules.** Three layers, one direction: `Views → Services → Models`. `Models/` holds the SwiftData models (`Recording`, `Person`) and pure, `nonisolated` value code (alignment, live labels, search, clustering, voice windows). `Services/` owns the work: one `RecordingSession` per recording, the app-wide `SpeakerIdentifier` queue after Stop, `PeopleDirectory` (voice fingerprints), the model-owning engines (speech, Parakeet, Nemotron, pyannote/WeSpeaker, LS-EEND, Gemma) and their downloads, and the search index. `Views/` is SwiftUI only. Nothing under `Models/` or `Services/` imports SwiftUI; `FluidAudio` is imported only by the services that run its models (`FluidAudioDiarizer`, `NemotronDiarizer`, `VoiceEmbedder`, `LSEENDLiveDiarizer`, `SortformerLiveDiarizer` and their shared `LiveDiarizerStream`, `ParakeetTranscriber`), MLX only by `LocalLanguageModel`.

**Who owns what.** `AppServices` (the composition root, in the environment) owns the SwiftData container, `SpeakerIdentifier`, `PeopleDirectory`, the live diarizer, `NetworkPath` (the network the downloads wait for), `AppLock` and the three model downloads, which it starts only after the Welcome page (`modelDownloadsAllowed`, `startModelDownloads`), and makes a fresh `RecordingSession` per recording. `MainView` owns the UI's few pieces of shared state: the selected tab, `SearchState` (the Search tab's text and tokens, shared with the Transcripts tab's pull-to-search and tag chips), `isBarMinimized` (the tab bar's state, reported by the accessory), and the card: `TranscriptCardContent` (the session, the transcript, the search context, the zoom source), `showsCard` and `isCardDismissing`/`pendingCard` (a request that came while the card was still shrinking away). The active `RecordingSession` lives in the card's content, so it survives the card being swiped away; the accessory and the card read it. Every other view owns only its own `@State`; the lists read SwiftData through `@Query`; the transcript card edits its `Recording` in place (title, tags) and calls the services for everything else (rename → `PeopleDirectory.name`, "Speakers: n" → `SpeakerIdentifier.setSpeakerCount`, delete). Views get what they need from the environment: `AppServices`, the model context, `openTranscript` (opens the card out of a row: `OpenTranscriptAction`, `.cardSource`), `searchActions` (open/close the Search tab) and the zoom namespace. All of it is `@Observable`; `@Observable` services publish only what views read (`SpeakerIdentifier.inFlight/refining/modelError`, `BetterSummaries.state`, the download states, `NetworkPath.kind`), the rest is `@ObservationIgnored`.

**Data flow of a recording.** Transcribe → `MainView.startRecording` makes the session, puts it in the card's content (the accessory shows "Preparing…" at once, the card zooms in) and starts it. `RecordingSession.start`: microphone permission → `AudioCapture.activateSession` → `Transcriber.prepare` (model load) → `AudioCapture.start` (file + analyzer stream + live samples + levels) → `Transcriber.start`; `.recording`. While recording, the transcriber's events and the live diarizer's snapshots arrive on the main actor and update the pure `LiveTranscript`, whose `LiveText` the card draws (`LiveTranscriptView`, incremental). Stop → `seal` → the transcriber's last words (≤ 2 s) → the `Recording` is inserted and saved → `.finished` → `SpeakerIdentifier.enqueue` (words, speakers, voices, recognition; then the vocabulary pass and the title on its background queue). The card turns into the transcript when `.finishing` is seen (`TranscriptCard.Turn`), and `MainView` swaps the content when `.finished` arrives.

**Concurrency domains** (Swift 6, `SWIFT_DEFAULT_ACTOR_ISOLATION = MainActor`, so everything is main-actor unless marked):
- *Main actor:* the views, `RecordingSession`, `SpeakerIdentifier` (its queues are main-actor tasks that await the workers), `PeopleDirectory`, all SwiftData access (one `mainContext`), `TranscriptSearchModel`'s bookkeeping and the segments reads (in batches of 8 recordings between frames).
- *`AudioHardware` (global actor):* `MicrophoneCapture`: AVAudioSession activation/deactivation, AVAudioEngine start/stop; `PlaybackEngine` (Listen's AVAudioPlayer and the playback category); `AudioSessionUse.recording` (a capture holds the session: playback starts nothing). The tap thread touches only the `Mutex`-guarded `CaptureTap`, whose closures are built in `nonisolated` statics (a main-actor closure literal traps on the render thread).
- *Worker tasks owning a model:* `NemotronDiarizer`, `FluidAudioDiarizer` (+ `VoiceEmbedder`), `LSEENDLiveDiarizer`, `SortformerLiveDiarizer` (Debug builds' alternative): one detached task each, requests through an `AsyncStream`, only `Sendable` values cross. `LocalLanguageModel` is an actor (MLX on the GPU). `SpeechAnalyzer` is Apple's actor. Parakeet runs on FluidAudio's executors (`AsrManager` is an actor).
- *Global executor (`@concurrent`):* the search index's folding and queries (`TranscriptSearch`), `DiarizerSelector.regroup` (the "Speakers: n" clustering), `AppLock.capability` (LocalAuthentication's `canEvaluatePolicy`, a synchronous call to the system's daemon). Under `NonisolatedNonsendingByDefault` a plain `nonisolated async` function runs on its caller's actor, so off-main work is marked `@concurrent` explicitly.
- *Possibly off the main thread by SwiftUI:* `Layout`s (`MetaLineLayout`, `DateLineLayout`, `WrappingLayout`) and `TextRenderer`s (`WordFadeRenderer`) are `nonisolated` and capture nothing: iOS 26 measures them on the render thread, where a main-actor closure trapped.

**Deliberately simple.** State is derived where it can be: `RecordView` reads the timer's end from the session (`recordedDuration`, set together with `.finishing`), the card's melt eligibility is one expression, the lists hold no closures (so SwiftUI skips them when `MainView` updates). The only state machines are `RecordingSession.State` and `SpeakerIdentifier`'s two queues; the card's `Turn` holds what happens between Stop and the toolbar hand-over.

**The five service protocols** (`Services/ServiceContracts.swift`):

- `AudioCapture` (nonisolated, Sendable; `MicrophoneCapture` runs on the `AudioHardware` global actor): `activateSession()`, `start(writingTo:analyzerFormat:liveSamples:levels:) -> AsyncStream<AnalyzerInput>`, `seal() -> TimeInterval` (synchronous, for the main actor at Stop: finishes the streams and closes the WAV) and `shutDown()` (removes the tap, stops the engine, deactivates the session, off the main actor). Every converted buffer is both written to the WAV and yielded with its cumulative frame position as `bufferStartTime`, so file time, word time and diarizer time share one origin. With `liveSamples` (a `LiveAudio`: the live diarizer's `sampleRate` and a continuation) the same frames are also converted by one persistent converter to mono Float32 at that rate (8 kHz for LS-EEND, 16 kHz for Sortformer) and yielded in order, never dropped. With a `levels` continuation each buffer's RMS level (-50 dBFS → 0 … -10 dBFS → 1) is yielded for the meter (`bufferingNewest(1)`, about 10 per second). On iOS 27 the tap uses `installAudioTap(onBus:bufferSize:format:tapProvider:)`; everything the tap thread touches sits in one Mutex-guarded `CaptureTap`.
- `Transcriber` (MainActor): `prepare(locale:onDownloadProgress:)` (also loads the model: `prepareToAnalyze` runs before the microphone starts), `start(input:onEvent:)`, `finish()`. Events (`.volatile`, `.finalized([TranscriptWord])`, `.failed`) arrive on the main actor.
- `FileTranscriber` (MainActor): `transcribe(fileURL:locale:) -> FileTranscript` (the words after Stop with confidences and their `alignmentOffset`: seconds added to the diarizer's turn times when they are aligned with these words, -0.12 for Parakeet, 0 for Apple; the stored word times stay unchanged) and `applyVocabulary(to:confidences:fileURL:locale:)` (the vocabulary pass; every word keeps its times).
- `SpeakerDiarizer` (nonisolated, Sendable): `prepare()`, `diarization(fileURL:speakers:) -> Diarization` (exclusive turns, speaker indices 0-based in first-appearance order, and which model made them; `speakers` is `.automatic` or `.exactly(n)`) and `voices(fileURL:windows:) -> [Int: SpeakerVoice]` (each speaker's unit WeSpeaker embedding, from VoiceWindows).
- `LiveDiarizer` (nonisolated, Sendable): `sampleRate` (the rate its samples come at), `prepare()` and `start(samples:) -> AsyncStream<LiveSpeakerTurns>`. Each snapshot is the full state so far (turns in seconds, speaker indices 0-based by first appearance, plus `processedUntil`), at most one per model step (0.5 s of audio for LS-EEND, 0.48 s for Sortformer), plus a last one after the samples ended. Best effort: a recording never waits for it.

**RecordingSession** (`@MainActor @Observable`, one instance per recording from `AppServices.makeRecordingSession()`): drives `idle → preparing → (downloadingSpeechModel) → recording → finishing → finished(Recording) | discarded | failed`. Its audio work never blocks the main thread: `MicrophoneCapture` runs on the `AudioHardware` global actor (one for the whole app: activation, engine start, engine stop and deactivation stay in Apple's order even when the next recording starts at once), and `start()` awaits it, so the card zooms in on a free main thread. Stop seals the capture synchronously (`seal()`: the streams end, the WAV closes, the duration is known in the same update as `.finishing`, so the card's timer lands on it from the first frame) and shuts the engine down off the main actor (`shutDown()`), which is why the Stop → toolbar turn now starts on the next frame (Stop → first changed frame 115–130 ms before, 50–75 ms after, on the iPhone 17 simulator; the rest is SwiftUI's update, see Performance). While recording it hands every transcription result and live-label snapshot (at most two per second) to `LiveTranscript` (pure) and publishes its `LiveText`, plus a smoothed microphone level. `LiveText` holds the finalized words in paragraphs with their live speakers (`LiveSpeakers` labels the words, `SteadyLabels` folds them into paragraphs; both incrementally, row ids stay stable), then the provisional words. SpeechTranscriber sends its provisional text as one run over everything not final yet, so `VolatileTimer` splits it into words with estimated times (words unchanged since the last result keep theirs; new ones are spread up to 0.6 s before the range end by length), and every word, provisional or final, is shown under the newest live label for its time (none yet: with the word before). A different speaker therefore starts a new paragraph at once; the speaker's name appears once the label has held for 0.5 s (`SteadyLabels.hold`; per 0.1 s of audio for provisional words, `FrameSteady`), and a refresh runs when one comes due. The live screen (`LiveTranscriptView`) places the provisional words by those labels (the rules `SteadyLabels` applies once they are final, so firming up rarely moves them) and fades in only words that are new where they stand: a `TextRenderer` draws them at 60 % opacity at once, fully opaque within 0.2 s (each word fades over 0.15 s; words arriving together start up to 0.05 s apart), without blur or offset (a `TimelineView` runs only while something fades; with Reduce Motion nothing fades). On Stop (button, audio interruption or transcriber failure) it waits at most 2 s for the transcriber's last words (`finish()` normally takes about 0.1 s, but up to 47 s were measured on a loaded Mac; past the cap the provisional tail is kept as words and later results are ignored, since the full-accuracy pass re-transcribes the file anyway) and at most 0.5 s for the live diarizer's last snapshot, saves the `Recording` with its words and segments (labelled with the preliminary live speakers when available, otherwise un-attributed; `speakerStatus` stays `pending`), then hands it to `SpeakerIdentifier`. Empty recordings are discarded and their file deleted. Interruptions: `interruptionNotification` (began) on iOS 26, `didBecomeInactiveNotification` with a system deactivation source on iOS 27.

**SpeakerIdentifier** (`@MainActor @Observable`, app-wide) runs the work after Stop on one serial queue, one recording at a time (Parakeet alone is a ~600 MB model): (1) the full-accuracy words (`FileTranscriber.transcribe`; they replace the live words at once, with the live labels re-applied; "Improving transcript accuracy…"), (2) diarization (`DiarizerSelector`, "Refining speakers…"), (3) the final alignment (`TranscriptAligner.finalSegments`: no island smoothing, the turns moved by the words' alignment offset), (4) each speaker's voice from those final labels (`VoiceWindows` → `SpeakerDiarizer.voices`), (5) recognition of known people (`PeopleDirectory.recognize`), then `.done`: the user sees the speakers. Each step's start and end goes into `SpeakerIdentifier.progress[id]` (`PipelineProgress`) and the words a pass replaced into `recentChanges[id]` for 6 s, which the card's status line and flashes read. The diarizer's turns are stored (`Recording.speakerTurns`, with `alignmentOffset`), so the words can be re-aligned later without diarizing again, and so is the model that made them (`Recording.diarizerRaw`: Nemotron or pyannote; recognition is stricter for pyannote's speakers, see People). Only then, on a separate background queue, the vocabulary pass (`FileTranscriber.applyVocabulary`) improves the words and re-aligns them with the stored turns: speaker numbers, names, links and voices stay valid, and `.done` never waits for it (the whole-file Dictation pass is most of the post-stop time: 5.4 of 7.4 s for 5 minutes on an M5 Mac). Then, on the same background queue, `TranscriptTitler` writes the automatic title and summary from the final words (see Automatic titles and summaries). `Recording.vocabularyPending` survives a kill, so the pass resumes at the next launch. The live labels' speaker count no longer matters: the old `.atLeast(live count)` bound and live-speaker fallback are gone (on DiarBench the live count was exact in only 44/70 recordings, and the fallback would have replaced a correct Nemotron result with live labels). The "Speakers" menu in the transcript card's bottom toolbar (Automatic, 2–6) stores `Recording.speakerCountOverride`, resets the speaker names and re-runs diarization with `.exactly(n)` (the words are kept). `prepareAndRetryPending()` runs on launch and every return to the foreground and re-queues everything still `pending` or `processing` (offline first launch, crash recovery), every unfinished vocabulary pass and, when the language model is available, every transcript not yet described (the one-time title sweep). **Order:** a recording that was just stopped always goes first: its identification (words, speakers, voices) before the next transcript of the one-time voice migration (`refreshVoices`; known names follow once the transcripts people's samples come from are migrated, see People), its vocabulary pass and title before the next transcript of the title sweep. Both sweeps take one transcript at a time and start nothing new while a recording is being made (`RecordingSession` reports Record … saved or dropped through `setRecording`); the transcript a sweep is on is finished first. Debug builds log the order (`[Scribe] queue: …`). Deletion while processing is safe (`isDeleted` checks after every await); whoever writes the segments last aligns the current words with the current turns, so the two queues never leave them inconsistent.

**TranscriptAligner** (pure, `nonisolated`): each word takes the speaker turn with the largest overlap (nearest turn midpoint when nothing overlaps); for live labels, single-word speaker flips between two agreeing neighbours are smoothed (`smoothing: true`, the default); the final alignment after Stop (`finalSpeakers` / `finalSegments`) moves the turns by the words' alignment offset and does not smooth (on DiarBench smoothing erased real one-word turns: 2.43 → 2.22 % wrong words without it, and the Parakeet offset took it to 1.77 %); consecutive same-speaker words fold into one paragraph, split additionally by silences over 2 s. With no turns every segment has `speakerIndex == nil` (live labels off, undiarized recordings). Words ending after `labelledUntil` (audio the live diarizer has not processed yet) stay unlabelled instead of guessing; `keepingIDs` gives a segment the id of the previous segment that started at the same time, so the live list keeps its rows; after Stop `keepingIDsByOverlap` (the words pass, the speakers step and the vocabulary pass, through `finalSegments(keepingIDsOf:)`) gives the ids to the segments that overlap the old ones most, each id once, largest overlaps first, since Parakeet re-times every word and a split should keep the id on its larger part. Each set of turns is indexed once (`TurnIndex`: binary searches instead of a scan from the first turn). While recording, `LiveSpeakers` keeps the live labels up to date incrementally: a new snapshot re-assigns only the words ending after the earliest changed turn and the words in silence gaps whose nearest turn could have changed (same labels as the full pass; randomized equivalence test in the live latency harness, `LiveLat equiv`, 3.7 million states).

## Simulator vs. device

`SpeechTranscriber.isAvailable` is `false` on every simulator, so Debug builds running on the simulator inject `FakeTranscriber` (canned sentences word by word until Stop: provisional words lower case without punctuation, a pause over 2 s after every second sentence; it declares the device's Int16 analyzer format, because iOS 27's `AnalyzerInput` traps on Float32 non-interleaved buffers), `FakeLiveDiarizer` (two speakers alternating every 5 s of received audio, about 1 s behind, a snapshot every 0.5 s, so live labels appear; at the chosen live model's rate, so the capture's 16 kHz path runs too when Sortformer is picked), `FakeDiarizer` (two speakers alternating every 4 s, or `n` with "Speakers: n", so the offline pass visibly replaces the live labels, reported as Nemotron's; a fixed fake voice per speaker number, so a speaker named once is recognized in later fake recordings) and `FakeFileTranscriber` (finds no better words after 1 s; its vocabulary pass changes nothing after 2 s) under `#if DEBUG && targetEnvironment(simulator)`. The real `MicrophoneCapture` still runs against the host microphone and writes a real WAV, so the whole record → stop → detail flow can be exercised. Real speech recognition and diarization must be validated on a physical iPhone 12 or newer running iOS 26+; older devices (iPhone 11, SE 2) show "Not Available on This Device".

## After Stop: words, speakers, vocabulary

After Stop, `SpeakerIdentifier` (one recording at a time) first re-transcribes the saved WAV (`SpeechFileTranscriber.transcribe`), then identifies the speakers (next section) and marks the transcript `.done`; the vocabulary pass runs after that in the background. Better words replace the live words (live speaker labels are re-applied until diarization finishes); if the pass fails or finds nothing, the live words stay. It runs once per transcript (`Recording.wordsRefined`).

1. **The words** (before speakers). Apple's `SpeechTranscriber` without `.fastResults` by default; NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B "Ultra" via FluidAudio (`ParakeetTranscriber`, CoreML, on device, 25 European languages) once the user has downloaded it in Settings › Transcription ("Download Parakeet (610 MB)", any network; the finished download selects it) and while it is chosen and supports the language. The ~610 MB model downloads into Application Support/FluidAudio/Models only on that tap (`ParakeetModel`), never by itself. Parakeet's word timestamps and confidences come from its SentencePiece token timings. Apple as the default costs some accuracy and time: 11.0 % against 8.9 % word errors on read German (table below), 2.59 % against 1.77 % wrong-speaker words with Apple's words (DiarBench, Nemotron turns), and a pass several times longer than Parakeet's, so the speakers come later after Stop.
2. **Vocabulary terms** (after speakers are shown, `applyVocabulary`, background queue; the improved words are re-aligned with the stored speaker turns, so speaker numbers and names stay). `DictationTranscriber` with the vocabulary as contextual strings, merged by `VocabularyMerge` (only terms it recognized at the same time position; case fixes for exact matches). `SpeechTranscriber` ignores contextual strings (measured: identical output with and without).
3. **Smart corrections (removed in October 2026).** A background step after the vocabulary pass: first Apple's Foundation Models model on single suspicious words with code-side checks (no help on real speech, see the table), then Gemma 4 E2B rewriting the "Name: text" turns 10–15 at a time, checked and aligned with the words by `CorrectionMerge` and shown as a proposal to review in the card. Never measured on real recordings; removed with the Settings cleanup. `Recording` keeps its four properties (`correctionsPending`, `correctedUntil`, `proposedCorrections`, `correctionsAccepted`) unused, so the store's schema stays the same.

Measured on 80 real German clips with reference transcripts (40 phone calls to a bank from PolyAI MINDS-14, 624 words; 40 audiobook excerpts from Multilingual LibriSpeech, 1282 words), on macOS 27. Word error rates count formatting differences as errors ("1 km" vs "einem Kilometer", "gebe" vs "geb"), so character error rates (letters only, no spaces) are given too:

| Step 1 engine | Phone calls WER | Audiobooks WER | Audiobooks CER |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apple SpeechTranscriber (app default) | 11.1 % | 11.0 % | 4.4 % |
| Parakeet v3 | 12.3 % | 14.7 % | – |
| **Parakeet Ultra** (optional download) | 11.1 % | 8.9 % | 2.4 % |
| Apple + smart corrections (first version, Apple's model; feature removed) | 11.2 % | 11.2 % | – |

Parakeet Ultra transcribed the 15.5 minutes of audio in about 6 s on an M-series Mac (Apple's pass: several times longer). An earlier test with macOS's synthetic German voices showed 20 % WER and large LLM gains; those voices (all except "Anna") are formant synthesizers the recognizers handle badly, and the LLM gains came from fixing their garbled non-words. Real speech showed neither.

The vocabulary (Transcripts screen, Settings) holds your own words (`Models/Vocabulary.swift`). The live transcript does not use it. The built-in list of German consulting, banking and finance terms (the "Consulting & Banking Terms" switch) is off for the App Store launch (October 2026): one trade's terms don't fit a general app; the list stays in `Vocabulary.builtIn` for a later, optional word pack, and a stored `vocabularyUseBuiltIn` is ignored.

## Automatic titles and summaries

After the vocabulary pass, on the same background queue (`SpeakerIdentifier.describe`, never before `.done`), `TranscriptTitler` asks Gemma 4 E2B (see Better Summaries below) for a 2-6 word title and a 2-3 sentence summary in the app's language (German when the iPhone is German, English otherwise: `TranscriptTitler.appLocale`), whatever language the conversation was in, since the summary is read in the app next to its labels. On a German iPhone the instructions, the reminder after the transcript, the part instructions and the prompt headings are German (`germanTitleInstructions`, `germanReminder`, `germanPartInstructions`): until October 2026 they were English with "in German" and the language was the transcript's detected one (NaturalLanguage), and German users got English summaries. The labels stay parseable (`Titel`, `Zusammenfassung`). Without Gemma (not downloaded, switched off, or an iPhone under 8 GB) there are no automatic titles and summaries: the transcript keeps "New Transcript N".

**Why not Apple's model (removed October 2026).** Until then Apple's Foundation Models model wrote them when Gemma was missing. iOS 27 has two on-device variants and the system picks one (`SystemLanguageModel.variant` is read-only): AFM 3 Core (3B) on 8 GB iPhones such as the iPhone 17, AFM 3 Core Advanced (20B sparse) only on 12 GB devices (iPhone 17 Pro/Pro Max/Air, M3+ Macs); adapters are obsoleted in the iOS 27 SDK. Core Advanced (an M5 Mac) lost to Gemma in the bench below, and Core on the iPhone 17 invented whole meetings from a transcript that was only "Hallo hallo hallo": "Thomas diskutiert die Finanzierungsmöglichkeiten für das Neubauprojekt" / "Finanzierung Neubauprojekt" (Core Advanced answered "Begrüßung" / "Person grüßt mehrfach." to the same prompt). Apple's Private Cloud Compute model would send transcripts off the phone.

The model reads the transcript as "Name: text" lines (the speaker's name, "Speaker N" while unnamed) and is told to name people but never write "Speaker N" ("ein Teilnehmer"/"a participant"). It answers three plain lines, "Topics: …", "Summary: …", "Title: …" (topics first: listing them makes a long meeting's summary cover more than its first part), parsed by `parseLabelled`. Long transcripts: parts are sized by the window, `TranscriptTitler.chunkCharacters(contextSize:)` = (window − 1,300 reserved tokens for instructions, answer and template) × 3.5 characters per token (below the measured 3.8-4.5): 65,450 characters for Gemma's 20,000 (`LocalLanguageModel.contextTokens`: about an hour of conversation in one request). Longer transcripts are summarized part by part as plain text, then title and summary are written from the numbered part summaries (repeated if those do not fit either). Code cleans the title (quotes, "Titel:", trailing period, openings like "Meeting über"/"Besprechung zum") and keeps it only with at most 6 words.

- `Recording.summary` stores the summary (also matched by search). The transcript shows it under the date line, folded into a "Summary" row ("Zusammenfassung"; the system's `DisclosureGroup`) that a tap opens (secondary text, all of it) and closes; VoiceOver reads the summary as the row's value, also folded. Opened from a search with hits in the summary, it starts open. Under it, in every transcript with or without a summary, "AI-generated content can contain mistakes." (caption, tertiary; "Mit KI generierte Inhalte können Fehler enthalten."; HIG › Generative AI: say where AI is used and that it can be wrong). It covers the whole transcript: the words, the speakers and the names recognized by voice are generated too, so the earlier caption under the summary alone ("Automatic summary · can contain mistakes") implied the rest was not. Search hits are highlighted, so a transcript found only by its summary shows why.
- `Recording.summaryVersion` marks it done once a model answered, refused or failed for good, so a transcript is described once. Without Gemma nothing changes, silently, and the sweep describes the transcript once Gemma is there. Likewise when Gemma is stopped (`TranscriptTitler.RetryLater`: the app left the foreground or a recording started): nothing is marked and the next return to the foreground tries again; a part summary stopped that way stops the whole description, so a title is never written from some parts only.
- The title replaces the name only when `Recording.nameIsUserSet` is false (any edit in the transcript card's title field sets it) and the name is still one the app gave ("New Transcript N", "New Recording N", "Untitled Transcript") or the title a model gave it before (`Recording.automaticTitle`, kept since version 2: a better model may replace its own title), checked after the model answered, so names typed before this version or during the wait are never overwritten. Titles written before `automaticTitle` existed can't be told apart from typed names and stay.
- Versions: `TranscriptTitler.version` 2 marks transcripts Apple's model described before October 2026, `localVersion` 100 Gemma's, 101 Gemma's in the app's language (October 2026: the sweep writes the older ones again, in German on a German iPhone). Transcripts should have `localVersion`, so once Gemma is there the sweep describes every older one again (an automatic title of Apple's is replaced too), and a newer `localVersion` does the same.
- Older transcripts are described once by the launch/foreground sweep (`prepareAndRetryPending`, newest first, behind any vocabulary passes). A recording stopped meanwhile goes first (it waits for at most the transcript being described), and the sweep starts nothing new while a recording is being made.

Apple's model was evaluated on macOS 27 with the app file before it was removed (scratch harness `titlebench`: 12 DiarBench meetings, 2 long ones, 40 real clips, 4 hand-written meetings): faithful but wordy titles and summaries, keyword-list titles on incoherent audio, 1-2 s per short transcript and 8-15 s for 8,000-18,000 characters on an M5 Mac (Core Advanced).

## Speaker identification after Stop

Turns come from **NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Diarization** (FluidAudio `Nemotron3Diarizer`, preset `c128-split-w8a8`: 98 MB + 2 MB, W8A8, Neural Engine graph, up to 8 speakers, 10 ms frames; `NemotronDiarizer`). **Prewarmed at launch** (`AppServices.prewarmModels`, once per launch, utility priority, never downloads): after every new build iOS compiles CoreML models for the Neural Engine again on first use, and the model used to be loaded only when the first recording's speakers were identified, so the first recording after a build waited for the compile. Measured on the iPhone 17 with `-warmupBench`: right after an install Nemotron's load took 35 s alone (178 s when other models compiled at the same time), afterwards 0.09-0.4 s, also in a new process; pyannote 2.5 s, then 0.1 s (it is loaded at launch anyway); Parakeet about 13 s, then 0.4-0.6 s (not prewarmed: it is optional, the fallback at most). A recording stopped within the first half minute after launching a new build still waits for the rest of the compile; meanwhile (`SpeakerIdentifier.isPreparingSpeakerModel`) the record accessory's second line says "Preparing speaker model…" (shimmering) and that recording's status line says it instead of "Refining speakers…". It starts at the first launch: iOS runs no app code after an install until the app is opened. The saved file is streamed through it in 10 s blocks (bit-identical to FluidAudio's whole-file `processComplete` on all 80 benchmark recordings, so a long recording is never held in memory whole). Each 10 ms frame goes to the most probable speaker slot when that probability is > 0.5, runs of one slot with gaps up to 0.1 s are joined, and a slot with less than 2 s in the whole recording is dropped (removes phantom speakers). `DiarizerSelector` uses pyannote (`FluidAudioDiarizer`, with `minSegmentDurationSeconds` 0.3, segmentation `stepRatio` 0.1 and VBx `warmStartFa` 0.1) while the Nemotron model is not downloaded, or when it fails to load or run. Nemotron has no speaker-count setting, so "Speakers: n" regroups its turns by voice (`SpeakerClustering`): unchanged when n is the count it found, whole speakers merged by average-linkage AHC when n is lower, and the speaker whose pieces split into the least alike halves (2-means) split when n is higher; pyannote takes n itself.

Measured with DiarBench (36 synthetic German meetings of 2-4 people from Multilingual LibriSpeech voices with normal, fast and overlapping turn-taking, each clean and in a simulated room, 72 recordings; Parakeet Ultra words; the app's own code: `DiarizerSelector`, final alignment, voices), on an M5 Mac. WDER = words attributed to the wrong speaker; DER with a 0.25 s collar:

| Post-stop path | WDER all | normal | fast | overlap | DER | right speaker count |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before (pyannote + live count + live fallback) | 16.9 % | 9.7 % | 29.5 % | 12.3 % | 18.0 % | 49/72 |
| **Nemotron** (automatic) | **1.77 %** | 0.46 % | 3.00 % | 1.94 % | 1.16 % | 70/72 |
| Nemotron, "Speakers: n" with the true n | 1.68 % | 0.20 % | 3.00 % | 1.94 % | 1.07 % | 72/72 |
| pyannote fallback (automatic) | 15.8 % | 11.0 % | 28.1 % | 9.3 % | 15.9 % | 46/72 |

Tried on top of the alignment and dropped (2026-10-05; scratch bench `build/qa/diarbench`: 12 meetings of 2-4 real voices from the Multilingual LibriSpeech German test set, read fragments cut at word pauses, normal / fast with short interjections / overlapping, clean audio, 5,017 words; the app's Parakeet Ultra, Nemotron and `finalSpeakers`, 1.82 % WDER): Nemotron's change already falls into the right word gap at 87 % of the true changes (one word off 8 %, two or more or missed 5 %), and the voice of a single word (`VoiceEmbedder.embeddings` in its 10 s context) finds the right speaker less often than the alignment does where it errs: at changes 91-96 % against 96.8 %, in interjections 80-85 % against 94.5 %, in overlap no better. So moving turn boundaries into the nearest or widest pause (best 1.82 → 1.76 %, 6 words fixed, 3 broken), re-deciding the words at a change by voice (at best even, 14 fixed / 13 broken; with lower margins worse) and re-deciding short runs between pauses by voice (1.78 %, 3 / 1) are all within noise. The errors left are mostly words spoken while someone else talks (37 of 91), which one label per word can't fix, and changes the diarizer misses.
Clean and room recordings score alike with Nemotron (1.76 / 1.78 %). Four 9-10 minute meetings with 7-8 returning speakers (clean + room): Nemotron 1.63 % (count 7/8), with the true n 1.51 % (8/8), pyannote 11.5 % (4/8). Clustering all pieces from scratch for "Speakers: n" (the first design) scrambled 4 of these 8 recordings (10.6 %), hence the regrouping above. Its span embeddings read their 10 s of audio one Core ML batch (16 spans) at a time, not all at once (640 KB per span, 1,000+ spans in an hour): on the 9.3-minute meeting peak memory fell from 700 to 577 MB (max RSS 246 → 166 MB), the same as without regrouping, with the same result. Runtime on the M5 (diarization + voices, interleaved and repeated): 0.19 s per audio minute with Nemotron (short and long meetings alike), 0.48-0.56 s with pyannote. Not measured: an iPhone (Neural Engine compile time and memory of this preset; the measured alternative is preset `offline`, GPU, 190 MB, 1.65 %: change `presetName`), real meeting recordings (the benchmark is read speech cut into turns), more than 8 speakers.

## People and voice fingerprints

**Recognize People by Voice** (Settings › People, the first group, and the onboarding's voice page; `PeopleDirectory.recognitionKey`). **Off by default** since October 2026: a voice profile identifies a person, which makes it biometric data (GDPR Art. 9), so it is opt-in; someone who already had voice profiles keeps it on (AppServices, once). UI tests run with it on (`-voiceRecognitionOn`, ScribeUITestCase's default; `-resetVoiceRecognition` gives a new install's state). Off, we keep no voice profiles and recognize no one: `SpeakerIdentifier` computes no speaker voices after Stop (their `voicesVersion` is set current, so turning it on later doesn't compute them for transcripts made while it was off), naming a speaker stores no sample, `recognize`/`recognizeEverywhere` and the voice migration (`refreshVoices`) do nothing, and the rename sheet has no "Similar voice". Speakers are still told apart (diarization) and can be named by hand. Turning it off while profiles exist (`PeopleDirectory.hasVoiceProfiles`, checked after the tap's frame) asks first ("Delete Voice Profiles?": Turn Off and Delete / Cancel), then `deleteAllVoiceProfiles` clears every person's samples and fingerprint and every transcript's speaker voices; people and the names in transcripts stay. Turned on again, it works from the next recording; a person's voice is known again once they are named in a transcript recorded with it on. The onboarding's voice page ("Recognize People by Voice": Know Who Is Speaking; Biometric Data, "A voice profile identifies a person. Turn this on only if the people you record agree."; Never Leaves This iPhone; two large buttons, "Recognize People by Voice" and "No Thanks") asks once, and the privacy policy says it is off until turned on.

Naming a speaker in a transcript links it to a **Person** of that name (`Models/Person.swift`, a SwiftData model, created on first use) and stores that speaker's voice embedding as a sample; the person's fingerprint is the normalized mean of their samples. After each diarization, `PeopleDirectory.recognize` gives unnamed speakers the name of a person whose fingerprint matches (cosine similarity ≥ 0.68 for Nemotron's speakers, ≥ 0.75 for pyannote's and for transcripts from before Feder, with a 0.05 lead over the runner-up, one-to-one per transcript). Naming someone also recognizes them in older transcripts where their speaker is still unnamed. Recognition never adds samples; only names the user types do, so a wrong match cannot drift a fingerprint, and renaming a wrongly recognized speaker moves that speaker's sample to the right person.

**Discoverability**: nothing showed that the bold "Speaker 1" is a button, so the first transcript whose speakers are final and still all unnamed shows `NameSpeakersTip` (TipKit `popoverTip` on the first speaker's name itself, so its arrow points at the name: "Name the Speakers", tap a name to change it, plus "Feder then recognizes them by voice in later recordings" while recognition is on). It shows once: a rename (tap or the long-press menu) invalidates it, and closing it ends it too. The page behind it stays live (`tipBackgroundInteraction(.enabled)`, iOS 26), so the first tap on the name renames at once; without it the popover took that tap to close itself and naming took two taps the first time. Debug builds' Reset Onboarding shows it again at once: TipKit keeps a tip's state by its id, and the reset counts a generation into the id (`NameSpeakersTip.showAgain`), since `Tips.resetDatastore()` only works before `Tips.configure` at launch. **Renaming** (the sheet from a name row, `Views/SpeakerNameSheet.swift`): the name field is focused and prefilled with the current name, selected, so typing replaces it; Save (✓, or Return) names the speaker, a blank name or "Use Default Name" goes back to "Speaker N", Cancel (✕) or swiping the sheet down changes nothing. Below it the known people as large rows: the person this speaker is linked to (checkmark), then people whose fingerprint is like this speaker's voice ("Similar voice", cosine ≥ 0.60, `VoiceMatch.suggestion`: only an ordering hint, never applied by itself), then everyone else by name with the number of their transcripts ("Already in this transcript" for another speaker's person). Typing filters them as search suggestions match (name prefix, word prefix, then substring from 3 letters), the typed part bold (`SpeakerNameChoices`). Choosing a person names the speaker with that person's name, which also confirms a recognized speaker: its voice becomes one of the person's samples. The sheet replaces the earlier inline field, which was easy to miss (see UI tests) and lost its focus.

The voices come from what the transcript finally shows, whichever diarizer or speaker count produced it: `VoiceWindows` takes each speaker's runs of words (no pause over 1 s, at least 1 s, at most 10 s per window, longest first up to 180 s), and `VoiceEmbedder` embeds each window with pyannote's WeSpeaker models (256 values), the audio repeated to fill the model's 10 s input (zero padding shifts the FBank features, which are mean-normalized over the whole input, and makes different speakers alike) with frame weights on the words; a speaker's voice is the speech-weighted mean of its windows, and when 2-means splits them into two clearly different groups only the larger one counts. They are stored per transcript in `Recording.speakerVoices` (`voicesVersion` 1), with `Recording.speakerPeople` mapping speaker numbers to people. On the first launch with this version, `SpeakerIdentifier.refreshVoices` recomputes the voices of every finished transcript from the labels it shows, replaces its samples in people's fingerprints (`PeopleDirectory.replaceVoices`), gives a sample to people the user named where the transcript had no voice for that speaker (the old live fallback stored none), and runs recognition everywhere again. A named speaker the shown labels give no window (none of its runs of words lasts 1 s: someone who only said "Ja." or spoke in fast exchanges) keeps its sample and its old voice, which is in the same embedding space (cosine p50 0.98 with the new voice of the same speaker), so a person named only there stays recognizable and renaming finds the voice again; unnamed speakers without a new voice lose their old one. The migration takes one transcript at a time behind any recording stopped meanwhile and pauses while recording; the transcripts people's samples come from go first, and a recording finished before they are done gets its known names right after them, from the migrated fingerprints. Choosing a speaker count in the transcript card's Speakers menu resets that transcript's links and samples.

Calibration (`VoiceMatch` in `Models/Person.swift`), on the app's own post-stop path in the DiarBench harness (Nemotron turns, final alignment, VoiceWindows/VoiceEmbedder voices) and the voicerec protocol: 36 + 126 synthetic German meetings of 2-4 people from 30 real voices, clean and in a simulated room; meetings in order, each person named once at their first appearance and recognized in up to 12 later meetings; 5 rotating holdout groups of 6 never-named people × 4 enroll → test acoustic pairings (1,248 + 5,568 tests of named people, 912 + 1,992 of strangers, up to 24 known people). The operating point is the threshold with no more than 1 % of strangers named on both sets, with a cushion:

| Threshold / lead | Named correctly (36 / 126) | Someone else's name | Strangers named | Shown names right |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.60 / 0.05 | 97.8 / 96.3 % | 0 % | 0.9 / 0.6 % | 99.3 / 99.8 % |
| 0.65 / 0.05 | 97.8 / 95.2 % | 0 % | 0.4 / 0.1 % | 99.7 / 100 % |
| **0.68 / 0.05** (app) | **96.2 / 94.5 %** | 0 % | **0 / 0 %** | 100 / 100 % |
| 0.72 / 0.05 | 93.3 / 92.5 % | 0 % | 0 / 0 % | 100 / 100 % |

A lead of 0.03 instead of 0.05 changed nothing. If the user also names every miss: 96.8 / 98.5 % correct, still no stranger named (at 0.60 1.0 / 1.2 %). 0.68 rather than 0.60-0.66 because strangers were still named at 0.66, the benchmark is read speech (real meetings give less pure speakers), and more saved people give a stranger more chances: the per-pair false-accept rate at 0.68 is 0.0095 %, extrapolated (not measured) 0.24 % strangers named with 25 known people and 0.5 % with 50 (at 0.60: 2.8 % and 5.5 %). With the pyannote fallback (until Nemotron is downloaded) 0.68 gave 82.4 / 75.6 % correct, 0.7 / 1.3 % someone else's name and 2.1 / 3.4 % strangers named (merged and mixed speakers; it reaches no 1 % point at any threshold up to 0.80 on the 36 meetings), so its speakers are recognized at 0.75 (`VoiceMatch.fallbackThreshold`; `Recording.diarizerRaw` records which model made a transcript's speakers, and transcripts from before Feder, made with the old pyannote pipeline or live labels, count as pyannote's): 74.1 / 65.5 % correct, 0.7 / 0.8 % someone else's name, 1.6 / 0.9 % strangers named (same sweep and protocol). Before (pyannote centroids, live count and fallback, 0.6): 62 / 54 % correct, 2.8 / 4.9 % strangers named. Old fingerprints: the old pyannote centroids are close to the new voices of the same speaker (cosine p50 0.97), but 16 % of named people had no sample at all (live fallback); after `refreshVoices` (new voices from the old stored labels) people enrolled in old transcripts are recognized in new Nemotron transcripts at 94.2 / 92.7 % (0 / 0.6 % strangers named), against 78.9 / 75.9 % with the old samples kept.

Settings → People lists everyone, searchable, swipe to delete; a person shows the transcripts they speak in. Deleting a person removes the fingerprint and links; names already written in transcripts stay, and so do the voices stored with each transcript (`speakerVoices`, one per speaker, named or not), so the privacy policy and Before You Record say to delete a person's transcripts too to forget their voice. Deleting a transcript removes its samples (and people left with neither samples nor transcripts). The Transcripts search also matches speaker names.

## Search

The Search tab (see Look) shows our search bar at the top (prompt "People, tags or words"); its close button goes back to the previous tab, and the text and tokens stay until changed. **Pull to search**: pulling the Transcripts list down past its top by 100 pt (rubber-banded: about 200 pt of finger travel) and lifting the finger there opens the Search tab (only on the Transcripts tab's plain list; also while a recording runs minimized in the accessory), with a light haptic once lifting will open it; pushing back up below the threshold before lifting, or a bounce after a fling, does nothing. The moment the finger lifts comes from `ScrollDragObserver` (`Views/ScrollDragObserver.swift`), which adds a target to the List's own `UIScrollView.panGestureRecognizer` (scrolling, taps and swipe actions are untouched): SwiftUI's `onScrollPhaseChange` stays `.idle` for a List that fits on screen (measured on iOS 27). (The progress ring around the former top-right Search button went with that button.) **Debounce**: what is typed is searched 0.3 s after the last keystroke (`RecordingsListView.typingPause`), and results, suggestions, the "n Transcripts" header and highlights all follow that text, so the list doesn't churn per keystroke; clearing the field, choosing a suggestion and adding or removing tokens apply at once. What is typed is matched in titles, places, tags, speaker names, summaries and the transcripts' words; every word must match somewhere (AND). Matching ignores case, accents and character width ("muller" finds Müller, "strasse" finds Straße); words under 3 characters match only at word starts ("an" finds "Anna", not "Stand"), longer ones also inside words (German compounds: "vertrag" finds "Arbeitsvertrag"); `#bud` typed as text matches tags only.

- **Suggestions**: while searching, the first list section (our own, not `.searchSuggestions`, which hides the results while typing) shows up to 8 people and tags as compact chips that wrap into lines (`WrappingLayout`; icon, name with the matched part bold, number of transcripts) matching the last typed word or the whole text (equal, prefix, word prefix inside the name, then substring from 3 characters), the matched part bold, with the number of transcripts (carried by each `Suggestion`); with an empty field the most used people and tags, half of each (the rest from the other kind when one has fewer); tokens already chosen are left out, and the suggestions stay after one is chosen. `#` suggests only tags, `@` only people. Choosing one replaces the typed fragment with a **token** (person: a named speaker must equal it; tag: the transcript must have it). A tag chip's tap on a row adds a tag token too.
- **Results** (newest first, "n Transcripts" header): the title with its hits highlighted, the matched people as filled chips, the tags (matched ones filled), and a two-line excerpt of the paragraph with the most search words, around its first hit (word-start hits preferred), with its speaker's name; a person token alone shows the first thing that person said; a hit only in the summary shows that part of the summary. Highlights use the accent colour. Without a query the list is the plain one.
- **Opened from a result** the transcript highlights the search words (in its summary under the date line too) and the matched speakers' names, starts at the result's paragraph (marked with a light accent background) and steps through the paragraphs with hits from the card's bottom toolbar ("3 of 12" with previous/next, which wrap around, between a More menu and Delete; see Transcript card). While the navigator is there, Share, Add Tag and Speakers share that More menu: seven items fit no iPhone's bar (iOS moved Next Match into an overflow menu on the iPhone 17, the iPhone Air and the iPhone 18 Pro Max, iOS 27 simulators; before Add Tag six fit from 402 pt on); with only a person token it steps through that person's turns.
- **Index** (`TranscriptSearchModel`): nothing is indexed before search is first presented. Then each recording becomes a `SearchDocument` (its paragraphs lowercased and accent-folded once into one UTF-8 buffer that `memmem` scans); afterwards only recordings whose `Recording.searchKey` changed are rebuilt. The key hashes name, tags, place, summary, speaker names, speaker status, `wordsRefined`, `vocabularyPending` and the speaker count, which change whenever the segments are rewritten, so the large `segments` are not read to detect changes (a new writer of segments must change one of them too). Reading the segments happens on the main actor (SwiftData); folding and every query run off it (`@concurrent`), 80 ms after the last change of the searched text (which itself trails typing by 0.3 s), a newer query cancelling the older one. Highlight ranges are found again in the original text, since folding can change lengths (ß → ss).
- Measured on an M5 Mac (500 transcripts × 10,000 words, 30.8 MB; search lab benchmark): index 0.5 ms per transcript (252 ms for 500), queries 15-40 ms (78 ms for two words), 10× faster than `localizedStandardContains` on a miss. Not measured: an iPhone, and how long SwiftData takes to load the segments of 500 long transcripts when search is first opened.

## Microphone

Recording always uses the iPhone's built-in microphone, even with AirPods or another headset connected. Bluetooth headset microphones run at telephony quality (24 kHz) and, in testing on device, produced audio the transcriber returned no text for.

## First-launch downloads

Processing is fully on-device. Only the models are fetched once (all FluidAudio models are excluded from iCloud/iTunes backup), and nothing is fetched before the Welcome page has said what and how much (App Review 4.2.3(ii); `AppServices.modelDownloadsAllowed`). Existing users (any transcript, Nemotron already on the phone, or Gemma's page already seen; `AppServices.migrateExistingUser`, decided once on the first launch of this version) count as past it. The network is watched for the app's lifetime (`NetworkPath`: Wi-Fi, cellular or hotspot, Low Data Mode, offline). It replaced a one-shot check at launch and foreground with three flaws: it took "no network" for Wi-Fi (an unsatisfied path is neither expensive nor constrained, so Nemotron tried, failed and said "Download Failed"), it never noticed Wi-Fi arriving while the app was open, and it called Low Data Mode "Waiting for Wi-Fi" while the status bar showed Wi-Fi. Revisions: FluidAudio 0.17.4 pins the pyannote repo itself (`df2625ac…`) and fetches the others from `main`. The app pins LS-EEND to the Hugging Face commit tested on 2026-10-07 and Sortformer (Debug builds) to `ae9a27ab…`, `main` on 2026-10-11 and unchanged since 2026-06-24, the files the Mac bench ran (`AppServices.modelRevisions` → `ModelRegistry.revisionOverrides`): each model is one download whose `.fluidaudio-revision` marker sits inside the model folder (Sortformer's in its repo folder `sortformer`, which holds nothing else). Nemotron and Parakeet Ultra stay on `main`, because for a revision other than "main" FluidAudio deletes a repo folder that lacks its own marker (ModelCache.swift:43-45): Nemotron's loader downloads its bundle and then the repo root, so a pin wiped the bundle it had just fetched (a fresh install failed once and downloaded twice), and Parakeet folders from before a pin have no marker (existing users would have fetched 610 MB again at their next transcription). Nemotron is guarded by FluidAudio's weights-version marker instead.

- **Apple speech asset** for the current locale (`AssetInventory.assetInstallationRequest`), downloaded by the system the first time you record; the Record screen shows the progress. The language is the first entry of the iPhone's Settings ▸ General ▸ Language & Region list (`Locale.preferredLanguages`), not the app's own locale, so a German phone transcribes German.
- **FluidAudio diarization models** (~21 MB, pyannote community-1 + VBx CoreML models) downloaded from Hugging Face into `Application Support/FluidAudio/Models` after the Welcome page, at launch and on every return to the foreground (`prepareAndRetryPending`), on any connection: it is small and needed for any speakers and for recognizing people. Before Welcome `SpeakerIdentifier.prepareModel` reports it not ready without fetching, so a transcript recorded first stays pending and is identified after Continue. Always needed: their WeSpeaker models make every speaker's voice, and they diarize until Nemotron is there. This never blocks recording: offline, recordings stay `pending` with an explanatory row and are retried automatically the next time the app comes to the foreground, when the network changes to any connection while the app is open (`AppServices.networkChanged`), or via "Try Again".
- **NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Diarization** (~100 MB, `nemotron-3-diarization/split/Nemotron3Diarizer_c128_split_w8a8.mlmodelc` + two small `.bin` files in the same folder; `NemotronModel`). Downloaded automatically after the Welcome page: at launch, on every return to the foreground, and 2 s after Wi-Fi appears while the app is open (`NemotronModel.networkChanged`; the wait lets a network that just joined settle, since a captive portal or DHCP still running would fail it at once). It downloads only on Wi-Fi (not on cellular, hotspot or Low Data Mode). Off Wi-Fi, Settings ▸ Speaker Identification says why it waits ("Waiting for Wi-Fi", "No Internet Connection", "Paused by Low Data Mode"). Like the App Store's cellular downloads, it offers "Download Now Using Cellular (n MB)" for this model and the live model together (`downloadSpeakerModelsNow`; n = 100 + 45 while both wait). In Low Data Mode "Download Now (n MB)" asks once more ("Low Data Mode Is On": Download / Wait for Wi-Fi); offline nothing is offered. A failure off Wi-Fi goes back to waiting instead of "Download Failed". On Wi-Fi a failure is real (Try Again, any network; shown once neither model waits for Wi-Fi). A download started on Wi-Fi finishes over cellular if Wi-Fi drops (FluidAudio's session allows cellular and can't be configured from outside), so the copy says it "starts" on Wi-Fi. Progress shows in Settings and the accessory ("Model: NVIDIA Nemotron" once it is there). Until then, or if it fails to load, speakers are identified with pyannote. FluidAudio discards and re-downloads it when its weights version changes (its guard against a changed checkpoint, since it isn't pinned). Not downloaded on the simulator.
- **FluidAudio LS-EEND live model** (~45 MB, `ls-eend/dih3/optimized/dih3/500ms/ls_eend_dih3_500ms.mlmodelc` in the same folder; pinned, see above) downloaded in the background while "Live Speaker Labels" is on: after the Welcome page, at launch, on every return to the foreground, when the network changes while the app is open, and when the setting is switched on (switched off, nothing waits for it any more). A missing model downloads only on Wi-Fi, or with Settings' "Download Now Using Cellular" together with Nemotron (`isLiveModelWaiting` meanwhile). A model already on the phone is loaded on any connection or none (`LSEENDLiveDiarizer.isDownloaded`: its `coremldata.bin` is there and no `.partial` file is left; FluidAudio creates the directory before fetching, so the directory alone proves nothing). ## Live latency notes

- Live text: `SpeechTranscriber` is created with the `.timeIndexedProgressiveTranscription` preset (`.volatileResults` + `.fastResults` + `.audioTimeRange`). Without `.fastResults` the model returns nothing until about 11.6 s of audio are buffered, then one burst; with it, a volatile update arrives about every second (measured on a Mac with German speech: display lag median 4.7 s → 0.16 s, worst 11 s → about 1 s). `.fastResults` also produces the saved final words, slightly less accurately (one German test clip: 4.8 % → 6.0 % word error rate).
- The speech model is loaded in `prepare()` (`prepareToAnalyze`), so the "Preparing…" overlay covers it and the first audio is not queued behind it.
- Live labels run on a `.utility` worker with a 500 ms step (two CoreML calls per second, CPU only) so speech recognition keeps priority, and publish a snapshot after every step (0.5 s; it was 1 s). Debug builds log how far the newest recognized word of each transcription result trails the microphone ("text … s behind" in the Xcode console); expect about 1 s or less on device while someone is speaking. The "input" figure only grows when the iPhone's compute cannot keep up with the audio.
- Speaker labels: SpeechTranscriber finalizes only about every 15 s of speech (words final p50 8.7 s after they were spoken), and its provisional text arrives as one run. Labelling only final words left a new speaker's first words under the previous speaker for seconds. Replayed on 24 benchmark conversations (48 min, 876 speaker changes; the latency study's harness running the app's own `LiveTranscript` and `LiveTranscriptModel`, which reproduce its best configuration without a new model download exactly), from before to now: the right speaker shows p50 5.5 → 2.2 s and p90 16.9 → 3.1 s after a change (fast exchanges 4.7 / 15.8 → 2.0 / 2.9 s, normal 9.1 / 18.6 → 2.4 / 3.7 s); words ever shown under a wrong speaker 59 → 44 %, share of display time under a wrong speaker 30.5 → 26.9 %, label flips 62 → 50 per minute, words right at Stop 77.1 → 77.5 % (that is LS-EEND's accuracy, not latency). A 200 ms LS-EEND step (another 43 MB model) would gain about 0.16 s more. A name that had shown used to go away for a moment and come back, mostly at a new speaker's first words and after a pause (reported on the iPhone). The cause: a name depended on some word of its paragraph being confirmed at that moment, and every hand-over lost the confirmation for `hold` — the final result (a finalized word started its hold again), the final removing SpeechTranscriber's whole provisional run (the next speaker's words with it, until the next volatile result), and every re-timing of the provisional words (the hold was kept per 0.1 s frame of an estimated time). Now: provisional words have ids that last across volatile results (`VolatileTimer` aligns each result with the last by edit distance) and into their final version, holds are kept per word (`WordSteady`, `SteadyLabels.carry`, not taken over where the final word's own label disagrees), the run's words after the finalized ones stay, and a name once shown stays while its paragraph keeps the same first word under the same speaker (`LiveNames`). Words are still placed by their newest label, as before: keeping a held label until another one had held too (tried) held words under the previous speaker when LS-EEND's labels went back and forth, and carrying estimated times across a final (in the build of 2026-10-05 16:32) put the next speaker's first words into the pause before them; both are gone, and each run is timed from its own start again.
