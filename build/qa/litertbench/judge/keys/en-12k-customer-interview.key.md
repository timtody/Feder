# Key: en-12k-customer-interview
Language: en · Characters: 12413 · Speakers: 3
## Must cover
- Discovery interview: Dev (founder) and Hanna (research/design) of Larder Labs interview Maureen, operations lead of the bakery chain Crumb & Kettle (14 shops plus one central bakery).
- Ordering today: each shop manager fills a tab in a shared spreadsheet by 2 p.m. for the next day; Maureen's deputy Priya merges the 14 tabs by hand, about 2 hours every day (Sundays included).
- Managers order by gut feeling; weather and local events (farmers markets, football, school holidays) drive demand but the spreadsheet ignores them; sourdough sells out by noon most Saturdays in the bigger shops.
- Waste is 8% overall (12% at Harbour Street, the worst shop), about £3,500 a week at cost.
- Tillpoint's included stock module was tried for about two months in 2024 and abandoned: it counts units, not batches/trays (e.g. suggested 37 baguettes when trays hold 24), so managers stopped trusting it within a week.
- Trust is decisive: Maureen wants a per-shop daily suggestion with a "here's why" explanation, editable by the manager, rounded to trays/batches, sent to central already merged.
- She rejects automatic ordering from suppliers: a human must always press send (an earlier typo brought ten times the cream); suggesting or drafting orders is fine. The supplier side is not her main pain anyway.
- Constraints: cheap tablets (about £120 each), poor Wi-Fi in some shops (Mill Lane in a basement), must load fast, no extra login.
- Willingness to pay: £50–60 per shop per month (about £840 for 14 shops at £60); above £1,000 a month needs the finance director Graham, who wants to see waste drop in their own numbers first.
- Pilot agreed in principle: 3 shops (Harbour Street, Mill Lane, Station Road) for 6 weeks.
- Central bakery capacity is about 2,400 loaves a night; Priya cuts orders by feel when shops ask for more. Capacity limits are useful but explicitly out of scope for the pilot.
